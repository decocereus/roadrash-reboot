# Tracks

`packages/track` compiles a `TrackPlan` into a `Track`. A plan has a start pose, a list of pieces, its region, and its traffic per kilometre. Compilation samples the centre line every metre, scatters trees, poles, and rocks from the plan's seed, and stands chevron boards along the plan's `chevrons` runs. The simulation and the renderer read the same compiled track.

`alignment.ts` owns the piece format and its sampler. The start pose is an `x`, a `z` and a heading. Heading is a compass bearing in radians, clockwise from north, with `x` east and `z` south, so a plan placed on real coordinates uses easting and minus northing. The lead-in runs straight back from the start along that heading. Each piece has a length, an end curvature, an end grade, an eased turn and an eased climb. A piece starts at the curvature and grade the one before it ended at, and the first starts straight and level. Inside a piece curvature and grade change linearly from start to end, so a piece whose ends differ is a clothoid and one whose ends match is an arc or a steady grade. The eased turn adds curvature on a 30% smoothstep ramp in and out that integrates to that turn in radians. The eased climb adds a half-cosine of height that integrates to that climb in metres. The last piece must end straight and level, because the runoff is straight and level. `eased([[length, turnDegrees, climb], ...])` writes pieces with only the eased terms. Every authored road is written that way and samples bit for bit as it did when a road was a list of sections. `clothoids([[length, endCurvature, endGrade], ...])` writes pieces with only the linear terms, which is how a generated plan is written. `pieceSpans` gives each piece's start, end and total turn and climb, which is what `placeHazards` reads to find bends and crests.

A plan says how wide a lane is, `laneWidth`, which never varies along a road, and lists its `districts`, a non-empty tuple whose first entry starts at 0. A district has its `from`, its `lanesEachWay` of 1, 2 or 3, its `shoulderWidth` and its `vergeWidth`. A road of one district is one width the whole way. Where two districts differ, a 60 m taper lies wholly in the wider one, so a narrow district is narrow for its whole length, and throughout a taper the open lane count is already the narrower one. `compileTrack` refuses a district too short for its tapers and a crossing whose reserved stretch overlaps a taper. `districts.ts` owns this, and compiling gives per-metre arrays that are read through `roadHalfWidthAt`, `vergeHalfWidthAt` and `openLanesAt`, because the half width at any s is `lanesEachWay × laneWidth + shoulderWidth` for the district there. The compiled `Track` lists every lane of the widest district as `sameWayLanes` and `oncomingLanes`, positive x for the race's direction and negative for oncoming, each from the centre line outward, so a lane never moves sideways and only the number open changes, always from the outside. Traffic spawns in every lane of the widest district, the plan's `trafficPerKilometre` cars per direction shared between that direction's lanes, and the slower cars favour the outer lane.

A car stores its `home` lane. On a road of several districts its x is worked out from its s and its home, capped by `trafficReachAt`, which falls 30 m to a lane ahead of a taper and rises the same way after one. A car needs no merging state, so a stopped car never slides sideways, and a restore works x out again. It joins the queue of the lane it will be in 100 m ahead while it still occupies the lane it is in, so the follow logic files it single file and a follower in the old lane still sees it. The rear car of a pair opens a gap when either is off its home lane. Pilots choose among the lanes open at the rider and two seconds ahead, and the grid stands in the lanes open at the line. On a road of one district a car's x stays its home. The start is kept clear: no same-way car spawns between 70 m before the line and 260 m past it, and no oncoming car between 70 m before and 300 m past, so the oncoming lanes the grid stands in meet their first car once the pack is moving. Riding, props, the police verge, and the renderer all read the half width at their own s, so a wider stretch is wider everywhere. The renderer paints one road texture per distinct lane count, and a taper uses the wider one with the mesh narrowing across it.

There are three roads, one per region, and each has one layout. Slag Row has two lanes each way and Ridge Road has one; Breakwater Run changes between one and three lanes each way across its eight districts. `docs/design/tracks.md` has their sections and the reasoning. For launch, three of these shapes become real cities: Slag Row becomes Madrid, Breakwater Run becomes Mumbai, and Ridge Road becomes a Bay Area mountain road.

Two more plans, listed in `stretches.ts`, are generated from the Madrid corridor by `pnpm madrid:stretch`: `madridHook` in `tracks/madrid-hook.ts` and `madridGranVia` in `tracks/madrid-gran-via.ts`. Neither is a `RoadId`, so no career, save or menu reaches them; they ride only through `?ride=madrid-hook` and `?ride=madrid-gran-via` in a development build. Each carries `scenery`: a ground cover, grass or paving, world-space block outlines with a height and a lift, grass patches and water outlines with a level. The renderer draws a greybox for a plan with scenery instead of open terrain and roadside dressing. `docs/madrid-corridor.md#casa-de-campo-hook` and `docs/madrid-corridor.md#puerta-de-alcalá-into-gran-vía` have how each was fitted.

The lengths follow the original game's level-1 roads, which measure 8.0 to 8.9 km. A race takes the AI pilot 2.9 to 3.2 minutes alone on an empty road on the Wren and 3.2 to 4.1 minutes (median 3.7) in a full field with traffic.

A road file in `tracks/` exports its plan. A road has one layout. Difficulty changes who rides it and how much traffic it carries, never its pieces.

A plan also lists `carriers`, the `s` where each car-carrier truck on the road starts when the race starts. `compileTrack` turns them into `Track.carrierStarts`, sorted along the road, and a road with no traffic has none. A start closer than 300 m to the line or past the finish is refused. `createTraffic` puts the trucks last in `RaceState.cars`, so `track.carrierStarts.length` tells the simulation how many cars to check for a ramp under a rider, and it removes any car within 30 m of a truck. Trucks are in the first same-way lane. `docs/design/tracks.md` lists every start and how it was chosen.

A plan's `bazaars` lists the stretches whose footpaths hold a market. `placeStalls` in `stalls.ts` fills `Track.stalls` with `StallFootprint`s from the road's seed, each with a stable id, a variant, a centre and a size, one about every 7 m on each side, with the front 2.6 m back from the road's edge. It fills the stretch minus crossing boxes and a gap of 1.5 m either side of every `walkSites` station, so `compileTrack` places walk sites first. A stall takes out a tree or rock it stands on, through `givesWayToStall` in `track.ts`, and chevrons and poles keep their places in the strip in front of it. The setup digest lists the stalls, so a room refuses a client whose market differs. The rider's rule is `hitStalls` in `packages/sim/src/stalls.ts`. A rider faster than 14 m/s crashes with the `prop` cause, and a slower one drops to walking pace and is pushed toward the road, so a touch never holds anyone. A sliding bike, a thrown body and a rider on foot stop at a stall and are pushed out the same way, through `stopBikeAtStall` and `stopBodyAtStall`, so a remount starts in front of the row. A rider running for their own bike passes through, because a bike can come to rest behind the row and a body stopped at the front could never reach it. Rooms race the same rule, and `packages/sim/src/stalls-room.test.ts` crashes two seated riders into Breakwater Run's stalls and checks both end on the room's race, hash for hash.

A plan's `hazardsPerKilometre` places oil slicks, rocks, and potholes from the road's seed: `placeHazards` in `hazards.ts` fills `Track.hazards` with `HazardPlacement`s (a stable id such as `hazard-7`, the kind, `s`, and `x`), and `hazardSizes` gives each kind's footprint and height. Each placement also names its setting: where a bend turns in, its apex, its exit, just over a crest, or a straight where riders pass traffic. The schedule keeps hazards off the start and the finish, keeps every hazard on the lanes open at its s, and only checks that a hazard can be seen from 25 m, so it is never hidden until it is under the wheel. No rock lies where the road from 10 m before it to 40 m past it curves at 0.0015 or more, and a rock where it curves more gently lies at a lane's edge, clear of a bike on the lane's centre. The only lane takes no rock, and no oil where that same stretch curves at 0.008 or more, so a rider at racing speed survives whatever lies on it. Traffic ignores hazards: cars drive over them unchanged, and only riders meet them. Test plans set `hazardsPerKilometre` to 0. `docs/design/tracks.md` has the rules and every road's hazards.

`road.ts` lists `roadIds` in career order, and `RoadId` is taken from that tuple. `roads.ts` is the registry: `roads`, `regionOf(roadId)`, and `trackFor(roadId)`, which compiles each road once and returns the same `Track` afterwards.

`regions.ts` holds each region's name and prop mix. The prop mix weights trees, poles, and rocks in the scatter and sets how close the chevron boards stand, so Salt Coast's roadside has more rock than Lowtown's. A region's colours and light are the renderer's business and live in `apps/web/src/game/looks.ts`. The renderer decides what a prop looks like per kit: a tree is a round street tree in a city and a pine on open ground, and a rock is a barrier block in a city. Rocks are scattered from their own seeded stream, so a longer lead-in or more trees leaves them where they were.

Chevrons announce the bends a rider has to brake for. `corner-warnings.ts` in `packages/sim` audits every road with each signed bike, the Sesta and the Wren. At each metre the bike's limit is the fastest speed at which full lock holds the bend or, where the curvature changes quickly, the fastest speed at which the push changes by no more than full lock in the time the bars take to swing full lock. A braking envelope runs back from every limit with the brakes weakened by the lean, drag and grade, and a rider who leaves the grid flat out and tucked meets that envelope in braking zones. A zone that takes more than 20% off the approach speed is warned. Its chevrons start at the braking point, less the distance the approach speed covers in a 0.25 s reaction and the eighth of a second the brakes lose while they ramp, and run to the end of the bend, on the outside of the bend that sets the Sesta's speed. A metre either bike is warned of carries boards. The audit writes `packages/track/src/chevron-runs.ts`, one list of runs per road, through a file snapshot in `corner-warnings.test.ts`, and each plan takes its runs from that list. After a change to a road, a signed bike or the riding model, `pnpm vitest run packages/sim/src/corner-warnings.test.ts -u` rewrites the list. Chevrons are solid props, so a test plan that spreads an authored road and replaces its pieces sets `chevrons: []`.

The track tests compile every road. They check the lengths, the lowest launch speed against the crest tables, and that only the rail crossing launches below 160 km/h. They check each road's truck starts, and that a road with no traffic has none. They also pin the first 4,250 m of Ridge Road, the original track, by digest (curvature, heights, centre line, crests, every prop, and the rocks by themselves), and check that a longer lead-in changes the trees and leaves the 35 rocks where they were.

The offline Madrid corridor and its schema live in `packages/track/data`. Its ingestion schema and reader, `madrid-schema.ts` and `madrid-corridor.ts`, are Effect boundary modules. They are outside the deterministic track rules and are never imported by the simulation or renderer. `tools/madrid` owns source downloads and processing. [Madrid corridor data](../madrid-corridor.md) describes its units, source pins, bridge markers and measurement limits. `tools/madrid/src/fit.ts` fits pieces to the corridor centreline under a minimum radius and reports how far the fitted road leaves the street. Only the two Madrid stretches in `stretches.ts`, reached through `?ride=`, are built from the fit; no `RoadId` road is.

## Junctions and crossings

A plan's `crossings` lists the places where something crosses the road: a junction, which has a cross road, or a pedestrian crossing, which has none. `compileCrossings` in `crossings.ts` checks each entry and places it in `Track.crossings` with its box, the distance from its centre to its stop lines, and the stretch it reserves. A crossing must stand on a straight, level stretch clear of the start, the finish and the other crossings. Hazards keep off the crossings, and the props and city blocks keep clear of a cross road's arms, so declaring one moves nothing else on the road. Open-country ground lies flat under an arm. Each signal is its own clearance unit, so the chase camera hides only the signal it passes. A road with no crossings compiles exactly as it did before they existed. `signals.ts` in `packages/sim` holds the light cycle, and `signalPhaseAt(crossing, tick)` reads a crossing's phase from the race tick and the crossing's offset, with no race state; the renderer draws the lights from it. The setup digest covers `Track.crossings`. `docs/design/tracks.md#junctions-and-crossings` has the rules and every road's crossings. Main-road traffic stops for the lights. A car stops at the stop line on a red, and on an amber it can stop for at its normal braking, and the cars behind queue 2 m apart. A car too close to stop runs the amber. Riders and the pilot ride through the lights.

Cars on the side roads are `RaceState.crossCars`, built by `createCrossTraffic` and moved by `stepCrossTraffic` in `packages/sim/src/cross-traffic.ts`. A cross road's `carsPerMinute` sets how often a car sets out. Each lane holds three cars for the whole race, so the list never changes length and a car off the road only counts down its `wait`. A car runs its lane from the end of an arm, stops at the arm's stop line on anything but a cross green, waits for a five-second gap in main-road traffic at a give-way, and queues behind the car ahead. On a junction with both arms it runs straight across and off the far arm. On a T it turns into a main-road lane at the box and leaves the race once it has run 200 m and no rider has it within 200 m ahead, so it never disappears in view. A car's dice come from a seed of its own, `siteSeed(seed, crossing.id)`, so a road's cross cars shift no other stream, and `carsPerMinute: 0` keeps every car off the road.

Cross cars brake for a rider, a fallen body or a pedestrian in their lane and never for anything else, and hazards never touch them. A rider hits one through `hitCrossCar` in `rider-collisions.ts`. If the car's own sideways motion closes on the bike at more than 9 m/s, the rider is thrown. Every other contact goes through `hitCar`, the same speed-and-lean grading as main-road traffic. The pilot models the same cost in `pilot-forecast.ts`: it reads where the car will be when it arrives, offers lines either side of that spot, and when no line is open brakes just enough to arrive after the car has cleared (`giveWay`). When braking cannot help it rides through. While a moving cross car is in its forecast it holds its speed rather than accelerate, because the forecast assumes that speed.

Pedestrians are `RaceState.pedestrians`, built by `createPedestrians` and moved by `stepPedestrians` in `packages/sim/src/pedestrians.ts` from the track's `walkSites`. `placeWalkSites` in `walk-sites.ts` turns each zebra a road declares into a site and seeds unmarked crossings and jaywalkers from the region's `footfall`, clear of crossings, jumps, the start and the finish. A plan's `crowds` adds unmarked crossings along a stretch. A pedestrian is waiting, walking, flinching or down. Nobody leaves the road: a walker who finishes crossing waits on the far kerb, then crosses back. They watch cars and not riders: a signalled zebra is crossed on the main road's red, and a few walkers go against it when the road is clear, while everyone else steps into a gap in the cars. Cars stop for anyone on the road. Nobody dodges.

A cop who sees a rider run a red or knock a pedestrian down chases them first. `meetLights` and `reportOffence` in `police.ts` set the cop's `witnessed`, `updateChase` prefers it, and the rules are in [game-design.md](../design/game-design.md#8-police).

A rider who meets a pedestrian gets a softer rock through `bumpBike`: speed lost by speed, a sideways knock the way the bike leans, a small kick, and the landing rules judge the rest. Straight at normal speed rides on with a wobble. Leaned hard or very fast throws the rider, and the crash is a `hazard` crash naming the pedestrian. At 14 m/s or less it is a stagger, and a clip of under 0.2 m makes the pedestrian flinch and takes little speed. The pedestrian falls, lies for 2 to 4 s, gets up and walks on. The pilot sees a walking pedestrian at their forecast position on arrival, at a rock's cost, with `pilot-crossers.ts` holding the geometry it shares with cross cars. An unskilled pilot misses some, decided by a roll on that pedestrian's dice, so no other stream shifts.
