# Race rooms

A race room runs one race for up to eight humans. The room is the authority: it steps the race at 60 Hz and every client follows its ticks. `packages/sim` holds the room's rules, so the same code runs in a Durable Object, in the browser and in Node tests. Ticks are the only clock. Nothing in `room*.ts` reads the time.

`roomSetup(setup, humans)` seats one to eight different racers as humans, rides every other racer on the pilot and turns pacing off, because pacing pulls the field toward the player and a room has no single player.

`createRoom(setup, pilotFromTick)` in `room.ts` keeps one seat per human. `accept(rider, firstTick, controls)` stores a client's controls by tick and reports how many were accepted, late or more than `INPUT_HORIZON` (420) ticks ahead. The first control for a tick wins, so a client can resend its recent controls in every packet. `step()` builds the tick's frame from the seats and steps the race. A seat whose control arrived rides it, and a seat the pilot was riding takes the bike back on that tick. A silent seat holds its last steer and pedals without punch or kick for `HOLD_TICKS` (6), then coasts, then goes to the pilot after `AUTO_AFTER_TICKS` (30). From `pilotFromTick` the pilot rides every seat and no control takes a bike back, so a tab left open cannot hold a room and its bill forever, and the client predicts that handover. The room and the client take that tick from the manifest's `raceLimitSeconds`, six minutes at launch. `leave(rider)` hands the seat to the pilot on the next tick and ignores its later controls. Every `HASH_EVERY` (60) ticks the room hashes its race.

The server starts a race, never a seat: no packet can start one. When the lobby's 60 s run out, the host freezes one `RaceManifest` (`apps/rooms/src/manifest.ts`) for that race: protocol, build, content and track hashes, seed, tick rate, the rule (bike, road and tier), the roster with AI labelled, each rider's bike, the loading deadline and how many seconds the room holds a dropped rider's bike (`reconnect.holdSeconds`). `started` gives every seat that manifest and its rider. Each seat builds the race from it and sends a ready packet carrying its setup digest. A digest that differs from the room's gets `refused` with `content` and loses its seat. When the last seat is ready, or leaves, or 20 s pass, the host sends `countdown` with one start moment on the room's own clock and steps from then. The lobby message and every pong carry the room's time, and each client keeps the earliest arrival it has seen against that time, so it knows when news from the room's start moment reaches it. Riders on different links count down to the same moment.

The rooms Worker writes no invocation logs, because each incoming WebSocket message would be a log event, and a log event costs about 80 times its message's share of a billed request. `host.ts` writes a few JSON lines per race through `ports.note` instead, typed by `RoomNoteSchema` in `apps/rooms/src/notes.ts`. A room ID comes from the invite link and can run several races, so each race gets its own random race ID from `ports.raceId`, and every note, the `started` message and `RoomReport` carry it. No note holds a guest name or the room ID. `started` names the build, the road, the seed and the setup digest. `ended` sums up every rider who started, including those who left: where they connected from, how long they took to load, what the room saw of their controls and crashes, and whether their browser's stats arrived. It also counts the gaps between the room's wakes. `pnpm rooms:read` turns those lines into a report per race.

Each browser also sends the room a race stats packet every 15 s while it rides and when its rider finishes, crashes out, leaves or closes the tab. `host.ts` keeps the latest and logs it once, when the seat's socket closes or the race ends, so a socket that dies still leaves a report. The `left` note carries the socket's close code. It counts how far corrections moved the rider's own bike and why, the rider's crashes and how many first appeared in a correction, the time the race ran slowed below 0.95 of its speed (`slowedMs`), below half (`stoppedMs`) and above 1.05 (`spedUpMs`), the most ticks the client confirmed or replayed between two frames (`mostReplayedTicks`), how far ahead of the predicted tick its controls were planned on average and at most, counting controls planned while the delay was longer (`inputDelayMeanMs`, `inputDelayWorstMs`), the frames that drew the rider's own bike more than 1 cm off a steady ride, as `room-screen.test.ts` measures it (`surgedFrames`), the frames that drew any rival that way (`rivalsSurgedFrames`) and the worst such offset (`worstDrawnOffCm`), the round trip and the slowest frame, because prod has no other record of what a rider's browser saw.

`host.ts` also saves each race's tick packets to the `RaceTapes` R2 bucket through `ports.saveTape`, so `pnpm rooms:replay` can run a race again and compare its hashes. [observability.md](../observability.md#race-tapes) has the format and the rules.

`room-wire.ts` holds the two packets. A client sends an input packet: a kind byte of 1, the first tick as a little-endian u32, a count from 1 to 8, then four bytes per control, 6 + 4n bytes in all. The room sends a tick packet: a kind byte of 2, the tick, a count of handovers with a rider byte and a controller byte each, a count of controls with a rider byte and four control bytes each, and a flag byte that says whether a 16-byte hash follows. The readers fill reused records and return `false` for any packet that is cut short, has bytes left over, carries a steer byte of 128, names a third controller or names a rider twice.

`createRoomClient(setup, me)` in `room-client.ts` holds three races. `confirmed` steps only on the room's frames, in order. `predicted` runs ahead and is the race the browser draws. `predict(control, delay)` plans this rider's control for the tick `delay` ticks past the one it predicts, and steps the predicted race one tick with the control planned for that tick and a guess for everyone else: each remote human holds their last confirmed control without buttons, and this rider takes the bike back from the pilot. It refuses to run more than `INPUT_HORIZON` (420) ticks ahead of the confirmed race, the same distance the room takes controls ahead of its own tick, so every control the client predicts is one the room can take. `confirm(frame, hash)` steps the confirmed race and compares the room's frame with the guess for that tick. When they differ, the next `settle(most)` copies the confirmed race into a third race through a snapshot and replays it at most `most` ticks toward the predicted tick, while the predicted race rides on, and returns the ticks it replayed. Each call continues the replay, whether or not the client predicted since, so a correction still finishes after the race stops predicting at the finish. When the room's ticks reach the predicted tick during a replay, the client drops the replay and takes the confirmed race, which needs none. When the replay reaches the predicted tick it is restored into the predicted race in place, so the renderer keeps the same objects, and the riders it moved become corrections. A rollback of a normal lead finishes in the call that starts it; one after a hold or a room stall takes a few frames, so it costs those frames a little precision and no frame time. Replaying 406 ticks of a six-racer race took 1.3 ms in one call on an M4 Pro before the slices. A review of the first slices found a guard that replayed at most once per predicted tick, so a replay begun as the race stopped predicting never finished, and a correction of 3.4 m stayed undrawn. The strikes and hits a replay finds wait with it and are heard when it is drawn, the sound of the rider's own hit included, so a hit never sounds before the riders move into it. A hit a replay found only counts as shown once the replay is drawn, so when the room's ticks reach the predicted tick first and the client drops the replay, the room's hit still sounds, once. A replay that finishes while newer room ticks disagree starts the next one within the same allowance; until then the next step replayed the whole lead at once, 107 to 118 ticks after each hold in a two-window race. It counts rollbacks, resimulated ticks and desyncs, a desync being a room hash that differs from the confirmed race's. A desync is only counted, never repaired: `pnpm replay:engines` holds Node, Chromium, Firefox and WebKit to the same hash on every tick, so a desync is a simulation bug for the stats packet to report, not a state a link can cause. `writeInputs(view)` writes one packet of up to eight controls that starts with the oldest control not yet sent and fills the rest with recent unconfirmed ones, and returns 0 once every planned control has gone out, so a client that predicted many ticks at once calls it until it returns 0.

The client hears this rider's own events from the predicted race as it steps, and every other event from the confirmed race; `takeEvents()` hands over everything heard since the last call, a burst of the room's ticks included. `takeFights()` hands over each `attack` and `hit` that either race steps through, with the tick it happened on: the predicted race runs this rider's strikes and every AI rider's strikes on time, and the confirmed race brings the other humans' strikes and every hit a burst copies in without stepping it. A local race hands over its own fights the same way. The cast keeps one strike track per rider in `rider-fight.ts`. It ages every track once per step and takes new fights at the start of every draw, after any steps that frame ran, so a frame drawn between ticks, or after several, still draws each strike and hit with the race it shows. A rider can start one strike per cooldown, so a strike heard again inside that cooldown, from the other race or a rollback, is the same strike and plays once. A strike starts at elapsed min(N, contact), where N is the drawn tick minus the tick the strike started and contact is its ticks to the hit, so a strike heard late appears at its contact pose on the frame its shove lands rather than after it. A strike heard while another is playing waits and starts when that one ends. A `hit` cancels the strike its target was running at the hit's tick, playing or waiting, as the sim does, and leaves strikes that start later. The flinch starts in full on the frame the drawn race first shows a strike landing on the rider. A hit within one stun of the last one shown is the same hit moved by a correction and doesn't start the flinch again. A bump sets `lastHitTick` too but never makes a rider flinch.

The client reconciles every hit the predicted race shows with the room through the hit ledger in `room-hits.ts`, matched by striker, target and attack within 15 ticks rather than by the exact tick. A shown hit the confirmed race lands is settled. A shown hit that a finished rollback no longer produces, or that the confirmed race has passed by 15 ticks without landing, is taken back; a rollback still being replayed in slices takes back nothing until it is drawn: `takeFights()` hands over a `RefusedHit`, and the cast ends the target's flinch if it is still running for that hit and no other rider's hit on them is showing. A refused hit keeps its record until the room passes it, so a later rollback that finds it again shows it again without a second sound. The attacker hears each of their own hits once, when it first shows: in the predicted race as it steps, in a rollback that finds a hit the guess missed, or in the confirmed race when the prediction never showed it. A hit that a correction moves, because the room started the strike a few ticks later than the guess, is the same hit and is not heard again. A refused hit was already heard; the sound is not taken back.

`packages/sim/src/room.test.ts` races clients over simulated links in ticks, each with its own latency, jitter and lead, through the encoded packets. Two riders racing twenty seconds end on the room's race hash for hash, and each one's predicted race equals the room's at the tick it stopped predicting. A rider who goes silent is handed to the pilot exactly 31 ticks after the last tick it was heard for and takes the bike back when its controls arrive again. A rider whose lead is shorter than its latency loses its controls to the pilot and still agrees with the room.

## The room on a Durable Object

`alchemy.run.ts` declares a second Worker, `Rooms`, from `apps/rooms/src/worker.ts`. It is separate from the site so that a merge that leaves the simulation alone does not restart a live race, and so the room's bundle carries no React. `Rooms` answers `/rooms/:roomId...` by looking up the `RaceServer` Durable Object named by that room ID and forwarding the request. A malformed ID answers 404. `race-server.ts` serves the contract in `contract.ts`, an Effect `HttpApi`: `GET /rooms/:roomId` returns the room's status as JSON, and `GET /rooms/:roomId/seat` upgrades to a WebSocket, or answers 426 to any other request.

`host.ts` is the room's plain core, given a clock, a timer and a seed as ports, so tests drive it without a Worker. A seat joins in the waiting phase and every seat hears a `lobby` message. A seat states its build and wire protocol when it connects, and anything other than the room's own gets `refused` with `build` before it sits, which the room screen shows as a reload. The room's build is `RUSHLINE_BUILD`. `alchemy.run.ts` reads it once with `roomsBuild` from `apps/rooms/src/build-id.ts`, through Alchemy's config, which also holds `.env` and `--env-file` values that never reach `process.env`. It hands that one value to the `Rooms` Worker, pinned with `pinRoomsBuild` so the Worker and both Durable Objects read it at deploy and again when they run, and to the game's build as `VITE_ROOMS_BUILD`, which Alchemy inlines into the client. A deploy with no build, or with `local`, fails before it ships, and a Durable Object whose Worker has no build stops at init. Only `alchemy dev` runs as `local`. `rv` builds the game with plain Vite for those rooms, so it sets `VITE_ROOMS_BUILD` to `local` itself. `scripts/rooms-build.test.ts` plans the stack and checks that the game and the rooms get the same build. Tools read the build from the status route. A room sends a ninth seat `refused` with `full`, then closes it. The message carries the reason because a client cannot rely on seeing the close finish. A member who arrives during a race waits in the lobby for the next one. The host gives out riders in the order it holds the seats, which after a hibernation is the order the runtime returns them, so no rule may read a meaning into a rider number. It builds the race with `seatedRace(seed, seats, rule)` from `packages/career/src/room-race.ts`, which is the career's practice setup under `launchRules` with `roomSetup` applied, every human on one spec, every AI racer on the same bike with its own skill, and the humans on the front of the grid, and tells each seat `started` with the manifest and its rider.

`hostAfter` in `apps/rooms/src/host-phase.ts` decides every step of a room's race, and `createHost` changes its state nowhere else. The room is `waiting` with no race, `loading` from the moment `start` builds the race until the last seat is ready or has left or the 20 s deadline passes, `countdown` for the 3 s to the start moment, `racing` from the first wake, and `waiting` again the moment `room.over()` says so. A seat is still loading while it is `here` and its `readyMs` is null, and nothing else records that, so a rider whose link dropped during loading does not hold the countdown. A race's end is a move, not a phase: the room reads `waiting` at once, and each finished rider's 5 s to report belongs to that seat. The state names its race, so the load deadline of a race that has already ended cannot start the next race's countdown. Only a move builds a state, and a wake builds none. The status route's `hostPhase` says which of the four the room is in. Its `phase` stays `waiting` or `racing`, reading `racing` from `loading` on, because a tab on the previous deploy decodes the route with `decodeRoomPreview`, which fails on any other value and retries.

`roleAfter` in `apps/rooms/src/socket-role.ts` decides what each connected socket is, and `createHost` changes a role nowhere else. The host keeps one record per socket in `present`: its member, its last lobby walk and its role. A socket is `lobby` from the moment it sits, `seated` with its race and seat from the moment the race is built, and `reporting` with its seat from the race's end until its 5 s grace runs out. The host then writes the report, closes the socket with 1000 and puts it back in `lobby` until its close arrives. Only `lobby` sockets hear lobby messages and walks, only `seated` sockets hear race messages and ticks, and a `reporting` socket's messages only fill in its report, so they do not count as hearing from it. A socket that leaves has no role, and its member reads away in the lobby, unless it closed with `SEAT_LEFT` (4410), which the lobby's Leave button sends, and so does a room whose game failed to boot (`seatClosing` in `apps/web/src/room-handoff.ts`): then the member leaves the lobby at once, so the others see the spot free and a custom room's host passes on. Every other close keeps the member away, including the 1000 a seat sends after a race and the 1001 a reload or a closed tab sends. A seated socket that closes with `SEAT_LEFT` or `SEAT_QUIT` (4411, the pause menu's quit) leaves its seat: the rider goes to the pilot and the host writes the `left` line and the report. Any other close holds the seat for `seatHoldSeconds` (see below) and writes a `held` line and the report. Leaving while reporting writes the report. A member who arrives on a new socket replaces the old one, which hears `refused` with `replaced` and leaves the same way, except that the member is not away and keeps its ready flag, colour and walk. Each `Seat` in the race's `riders` outlives its socket, so `ended` sums up every rider who started. The status route's `roles` lists each socket's role in the order of `names`. The phase says what the race is doing and the role says what each socket is, so neither machine reads the other's state.

The tick loop is an absolute-deadline `setTimeout` chain: tick N is due N/60 s after the start, a wake steps what is due and at most four ticks, and a room still behind wakes again at once. The status counts wakes, wakes that had to catch up and the worst backlog, because `Date.now()` does not advance while a Durable Object runs and these are the only clock facts a room can report. Each tick goes to every seat as the tick packet. Control messages from the room are JSON text (`messages.ts`). A client sends binary packets, input, ping, ready and race stats, in `room-wire.ts`, and JSON text lobby commands, `ready`, `choose`, `walk` and `act`, in `apps/rooms/src/messages.ts`. A ping gets a pong with the room's tick, which is how a client sets its lead. A race ends when `room.over()` says every seat has left or finished, been busted or wrecked. Every seat then hears `ended` and closes with 1000, and the room waits for a new race. A seat that closes mid-race is held, and the pilot rides it once its controls stop. A lost link closes nothing, so every client pings from the moment its socket opens, and the host drops any seat it has heard nothing from for 10 s, in the lobby or the race, exactly as if it had closed. Without this the pilots rode a race whose riders had all gone to the finish, and no `left` line was written. On November 9, 2026 `pnpm rooms:load` against local workerd on an M4 Pro held 20 full rooms of eight seats on one thread with no missed control, 0.54 ms of CPU for each room tick and the worst room 3 ticks behind, and first failed at 28 rooms, where 4.8% of wakes had to catch up. Each race is its own Durable Object, so races spread over Cloudflare's machines instead of sharing one thread. At about 0.5 ms a tick a full race uses about 3% of a core, so 1,000 riders in 125 full rooms need about four cores across the fleet.

A browser joins with `/?room=<room id>`. `openRoomSeat` in `apps/rooms/src/seat-client.ts` owns the socket and the room clock, pings through `createPings` in `apps/rooms/src/heartbeat.ts`, and `pnpm rooms:race` uses the same client. `seatAfter` in the same file is the only thing that changes a seat's phase. A loading, racing or ended seat holds its race in the phase value, and `ended`, `left`, `lost` and `refused` are final. When the race starts, `createRoomRun` makes a `RaceRun` whose `view` is the predicted race, and the game draws it like any other race. `stage.me` names the local rider, so the camera, the HUD and the race log follow that rider instead of rider 0. The race log reads the confirmed race, so a guess the room later corrects cannot settle a result. It settles on the rider's own state in that race, finished, busted or wrecked, so a log that starts after a rejoin still settles a bust or a finish that happened while the bike was held. `createRoomClock` in `packages/sim/src/room-clock.ts` leads the room's tick by the typical round trip (the median of the last ten pings), plus how late ticks arrive, plus two ticks. Two pongs that arrive within 250 ms of each other although they were sent at least 250 ms apart were both held, so both are left out of the median: they measured a hold, not the route. That works in the lobby too, before any tick arrives. A hold in the room's ticks is a gap of 250 ms or more, the first tick counted from the race's start moment, with no other such gap in the 5 s before it. Gaps that come back within 5 s are how the link delivers, a proxy that hands packets over in batches for instance, so they count as lateness and the clock follows them. It places the room's tick by the earliest tick heard in the last second, so a stream the room sends slower than 60 a second pulls the guess back within a second instead of leaving it to run ahead. Each second's lateness is the spread between that earliest tick and the 90th-percentile latest in the same second, and the lead uses the median of the last five seconds that heard ticks, so one second of ticks arriving in a burst while the race loads moves nothing, and lateness that lasts three seconds raises the lead. Ticks that arrive within a second of the end of a hold are a held queue draining, so they count for neither the lateness nor the room's tick, and the clock keeps the room's tick it had before the hold, or the one the race's start moment gives. A third review found every gap counted as a hold: a proxy that batched packets every 300 ms from a room sending 57 ticks a second kept the clock's guess of the room's tick from moving for four minutes, and the lead grew to 400 ticks until the race slowed to 70% of its speed. A two-window race through `rooms:lag` with a 2.5 s hold both ways every 15 s found the race ran below 0.95 of its speed for 22 s and 35 s of about 75 s: each hold left five stuck pings in the median and a second of burst in the lateness, which raised the lead to its 88-tick cap for a few seconds and then lowered it again. Leaving out the stuck pings and taking the median of the seconds halved that. The rest came from the held queue draining over about a second and from the target wobbling with each lost packet. With the draining ticks left out and the slack, the same race ran slowed for 52 ms and 0 ms of 60 s. Later runs of that race measured up to 3.3 s slowed and 3.5 s sped up per rider. Sampling the pace every frame put nearly all of it in the first ten seconds: a hold that caught the lobby's last pings and the race's first ticks left five held pings in the median and placed the room's tick by the first held tick, so the lead overshot and then came back down. With bunched pongs left out and the race's start moment standing in for the last tick heard, the same race ran slowed for 0 ms and 0 ms and sped up for 0.3 and 0.4 s, while the lead grew at the start. Until November 6, 2026 a tracker measured lateness against the clock's long-lived arrival line and fell 3 ms a second: a loading stall at the start left the lead 13 to 15 ticks too high 40 s into a production race, and rooms held each control 150 to 290 ms. A tick the room ran without this rider's control (`lostControls()` on the room client) raises the lead by two ticks for each run of controls sent before the last raise, and the raise fades to nothing over 20 s, slowly enough for the pace to follow it. A single slow ping raises nothing: the pace can move the lead by at most six ticks a second, so chasing a spike that is gone in a second only surged the race, and a round trip that stays slower becomes the median within five pings. A button press the room rode none of the ticks of is queued and sent again: from the next tick the client predicts, it holds that button until its own race starts the strike, or for at most a second, then moves to the next missed press. So a press made as the link slows still lands, two presses a stall swallowed both land, and the presser may see their own strike twice. The stats line counts these presses as `retriedPresses`, so a real race shows how often that happens. Only controls predicted before a confirm count as lost for the lead, so ticks a paused browser never predicted go through the catch-up path instead. One late packet moves nothing, because the earliest tick in the second still stands. The clock never steps whole ticks to correct itself: the browser's fixed tick runs on wall time multiplied by the clock's `pace` and draws between two neighbouring ticks, and the clock asks for at most one step per fixed tick. `pnpm rooms:race` and `pnpm rooms:load` seats have no screen, so `predictSteps` steps them straight to the clock's target. A target that rises is met by sending controls ahead, not by running the race faster. `inputDelay` is how far the predicted tick is behind the target, up to `MOST_INPUT_DELAY_TICKS` (30 ticks, 500 ms), and the room client plans each control that many ticks past the tick it predicts (`predict(control, delay)`). The ticks a growing delay skips hold the last planned steer and throttle without its button presses. When the delay shrinks the controls already planned stand, since some have gone to the room. The client then drops a later sample instead of sending it, but never one that presses a button it was not holding before, and at most one in `SHRINK_EVERY_TICKS` (12 ticks), so a shrinking delay moves a press at most one tick earlier for every 12 ticks since the last drop and two presses made apart still reach the room as two. Your bike then answers your controls that many ticks later, so `pace` pays the delay back: it aims at 2% faster or slower for each tick between the predicted tick and the target, trimmed to 2% either way, which the eye does not see. It steers harder, by 2% a tick held within 10%, only past the band the delay covers: when the predicted tick falls more than 30 ticks behind the target or within six ticks of the room's, or runs more than 20 ticks past the target or past the room's tick plus `MOST_LEAD_TICKS`. The pace moves toward its aim by at most a tenth a second (`PACE_SLEW_PER_SECOND`). On the measured links the delay averaged at most 2.5 ticks (42 ms) a race and was 0 on a steady link and through holds and cuts; a round trip that rises from 150 ms to 400 ms and stays there sends 4.4% of controls late, where the pace alone sent 14.6%. Only a room whose own tick rate drifts, 54 or 57 ticks a second for 10 to 20 s, moves the race past 2%, because the client has to follow the room. Until the eighth review of PR #198 the lead grew only through the pace, up to 10% fast for a second or more, so a rising round trip surged the race on screen; before that the clock ran 1.5 times as fast whenever its controls would arrive late. The client predicts up to `INPUT_HORIZON` (420 ticks, 7 s) past the last tick it heard, while its lead over its guess of the room's tick stays under `MOST_LEAD_TICKS` (90). The room takes controls the same 420 ticks ahead of its own tick, so a room that stops stepping for a few seconds and then catches up four ticks a wake still finds every control the client predicted meanwhile. The window is the 300 ticks after which the client gives the link up (`LINK_DEAD_AFTER_TICKS`, which `DEAD_AFTER_MS` in `heartbeat.ts` comes from), plus the most lead, plus 30 ticks of slowing, so the race on screen keeps its speed until the client calls the link dead. A link that stops delivering ticks for a few seconds leaves the bike riding on at full speed, and when the upload still works the room rides the same controls, so the held ticks arrive with nothing to correct. Within 30 ticks of the window the pace falls in step with the room left, to 0 at its end. The window also caps a rollback, which runs in slices of 60 ticks. The held ticks themselves arrive in a burst: confirming 300 to 410 of them took 2.0 to 3.0 ms on an M4 Pro, so each drawn frame gets one allowance of `MOST_TICKS_PER_FRAME` (60) ticks, shared by the ticks the seat confirms and the ticks the client replays, however many steps the frame takes. Past the ticks the time since the last drawn frame brings, plus one, the allowance also ends once that work has taken `MOST_CATCH_UP_MS_PER_FRAME` (4 ms), so a machine on which each confirm costs more than the time share still keeps up with the room, and the client replays in chunks of 4 ticks so the seat can check the time between them. A confirm costs about 0.2 ms on an M4 Pro, so 60 ticks could take 12 ms there and several times that on a slower laptop; with the time limit, catching up after a hold takes more frames on a slow machine instead of longer ones. The seat keeps the held ticks past the allowance for the next frames and works on them at the start of each drawn frame, also one that takes no step, as when the race has stopped at its prediction limit and needs those ticks to go on, and the clock still hears each tick when it arrives. A tab that goes hidden tells the seat it stopped drawing (`stoppedDrawing()`), and a seat with no screen never draws a frame, so both confirm every tick as it arrives until a frame is drawn again. A rider who finishes, is busted or wrecks sends their stats once the held ticks and the replay are caught up, and a seat that leaves, a race that ends or a page that closes sends them at once, confirming whatever is held, because the race is leaving the screen. Until review of PR #198 the allowance reset with each step, so a 30 Hz frame could confirm 120 ticks and replay 120 more, and any page that had drawn no frame for 250 ms dropped the limit, so a stall on a visible page came back to an unbounded burst. Until review of PR #198 the window was the 90-tick input horizon, so a 2.5 s hold on ticks froze the race and then jumped the bike 60 m. A second review found the window at 300 ticks with the room still taking controls only 90 ahead: a 4.9 s hold slowed the race to 30% of its speed, and a room that paused stepping for 2.5 s refused 64 of the rider's controls and rode those ticks without them. `apps/web/test/room-pacing.test.ts` rides the real clock and fixed tick over the links measured in the November 8, 2026 races: a steady 150 ms round trip, a Wi-Fi link that holds every packet for 100 ms twice a second, round trips that swing past 400 ms, a round trip that rises from 150 ms to 400 ms and stays there, a room that sends 54 or 57 ticks a second, a proxy that hands packets over in batches every 300 ms from a room sending 57, a hold that catches the lobby's last pings and the race's first ticks, holds on ticks from the room of 2.5 s and 4.9 s, cuts both ways of 1, 2.5 and 4 s, and the `rooms:lag` proxy with a 2.5 s hold both ways every 15 s. On every one the race on screen never stops and never jumps. It keeps within 2% of its speed on every link but the drifting rooms, which it follows within 10%, and the delay it sends controls ahead by averages under three ticks. The pace-only clock of the seventh review fails four of these links. Until then the clock added or held whole steps and kept its idea of the room's tick for minutes, falling 2 ms a second, so a room that sent 57 ticks a second for 20 s drove the guess to the horizon: the race froze for 614 to 871 refused steps per rider, then caught up four ticks a frame. `apps/web/test/room-screen.test.ts` rides the real room, room client, clock, fixed tick and correction drift over packets while the player and a rival weave: a steady link, 2.5 s and 4 s holds on ticks, 1.5 s and 2.5 s cuts both ways, and a room that stops stepping for 2.5 s. It checks on every frame at any speed that both bikes are drawn within 1 cm of a steady ride, and that the room misses no control the link delivered. A second check needs nothing from the drift code: the test follows each bike's own path, how far the predicted race moved it less every correction `takeCorrections` handed out, and fails a frame whose drawn bike moved off that path faster than a glide may, a tenth of its speed plus any speed a correction carried along the road, and 2.5 m/s plus any sideways speed a correction carried across it. Drawing bikes without their correction offset fails all eight rides on it, by up to 68 m in a frame. A frame counts the distance its drawn speed along the road changed by more than the bike's riding changed it, less any speed a correction carried, past 25 m/s² and the pace's easing, and the same for its drawn speed across the road, less any sideways speed a correction carried. A frame where the bike is down or getting back on counts nothing, and the next frame measured starts its speeds afresh, so a remount at a new speed does not count as a jump. A bike's riding is how far the race moved it in the race time the frame drew, scaled to the frame's own length, so a bump between bikes counts for nothing, while a jump, a frame that draws two ticks on, a pace that steps or a glide that starts at full speed counts. Two more rides brake through the outage, the player through a 2.5 s cut both ways and the rival through a 2.5 s hold, and hold the bike to the same limits. A frame where a correction changes a bike's speed is measured against the band between its speed before and after the frame. A glide at 30% of the bike's speed fails four of these rides. Until review of PR #198 the check allowed a quarter of the distance ridden plus 5 cm and looked only along the road, so a bike drawn 30% fast at 50 m/s passed. A lead 12 ticks or more past what the latest round trip needs shows the lag tag. Every rider a rollback moves, this rider's own bike included, and every rider a stalled browser finds the room past and rewinds onto the room's race, keeps the place it was drawn and settles onto the corrected one (`apps/web/src/game/rider-drift.ts`), and the camera follows the drawn bike. The settle closes the gap with a 100 ms time constant, but moves the bike along the road no faster than a tenth of its speed (at least 0.5 m/s) and across it no faster than 2.5 m/s, so a correction of a few metres never makes a bike look faster or slower than its riding by more than that. A bigger correction closes at the same speeds, so a 60 m correction at 50 m/s takes about 13 s and the bike is drawn off the room's race until then, a loss of precision rather than a bike that speeds up. A correction also carries the change in the bike's speed (`RiderCorrection.speed`), so the drawn bike keeps the speed it had, and the settle changes the speed it adds no faster than 25 m/s², about how hard a bike brakes. It carries the change in the bike's sideways speed the same way (`RiderCorrection.lateralSpeed`), so a correction that changes how fast a bike slides across the road eases that speed in at the same rate instead of snapping it. The glide eases into each correction the same way instead of starting it at full speed. A correction also carries the change in the bike's height, so a bike the room put on a truck's ramp, or kept off it, eases up or down within about 0.3 s instead of stepping. `placeDrawn` in `apps/web/src/game/rider-drift.ts` turns a bike's two posed ticks and its drift into the place it is drawn and the offset the stats count, and both the renderer and `room-screen.test.ts` call it. A player who braked from 57 to 14 m/s through a 2.5 s cut, while the room's pilot kept their bike at speed, sees their bike speed back up over about 1.7 s, and the speedometer reads the drawn speed. Until the sixth review of PR #198 the settle moved only the bike's place, so that player's bike went from 14 to 57 m/s in one frame. That happens only when the room rode something the client could not know: the player braked while their controls could not reach the room, or a rival braked while the room's ticks were held. A rider who is off the bike drops what is left. Until review of PR #198 the settle had no speed limit, and a 6 m correction moved a bike 2.3 m in one frame. A second review found the part past 15 m along or 6 m across still jumped, 59 m for a player who braked through a 2.5 s cut both ways. A third found that closing half the gap a second drew that player at 91.7 m/s on a bike riding 57.3 m/s.

A lobby outlives its races. `createLobby` in `apps/rooms/src/lobby.ts` holds the members, the rule, who is ready and the start timer, as plain data with no sockets, so any screen can draw it. Each member has a stable ID, and the browser's `rushline.memberKey.v1` in localStorage puts it back on the same member after a reload, a dropped link or a closed tab, when the rider opens the link again in any tab of that browser. A browser that refuses storage keeps one key for the life of the page, so a seat the page reopens is still the same member. Every tab of one browser is the same member, so a second tab that joins takes the seat and the first reads `You opened this lobby in another tab.` A member whose link drops stays listed as away for 2 minutes; one who pressed Leave is not listed, and comes back as a new member. The host is the oldest member who is here, so the host's seat passes on and comes back without anyone transferring it. Only the host can choose the road, tier, traffic and police, and a change clears everyone's ready. The race starts the moment everyone here is ready, or a minute after the first rider readied, and takes everyone here. A rider who sits down during a race waits in the lobby and rides the next one.

`seatedRace` scales traffic and police from the tier's own numbers in `difficulties`, so that table stays the only source of the values. Bikes are not a choice: every racer rides `rule.bikeId`.

After a race each rider's socket closes, because the client closes on `ended` and the room closes any left after 5 s, the time it allows for a late report. `Back to the lobby` on results reloads `/?room=<id>`, and the same member sits down again. A rider who loses the room mid-race gets their bike back. Each seat in a race is `here`, `held` since a moment, or `left` at a tick (`SeatPresence` in `apps/rooms/src/race-seat.ts`). A seat whose socket drops is held for the room config's `seatHoldSeconds`, 30 s at launch; the lobby message and the manifest carry it to the browser. The pilot rides the held bike as it rides any silent seat. When the same member sits down again while the seat is held, the host rejoins them: the socket goes straight to `seated` with `started`, whose `awayMs` says how long they were gone, then one catch-up packet (kind 8 in `room-wire.ts`) holding `Room.snapshot()`, the room's race written by the same snapshot code a rollback copies with, then `countdown` if the race has a start moment, and a `rejoined` line. `RoomClient.catchUp` restores the confirmed race from it and the predicted race restarts from that, so the rider draws the room's race at the room's tick, and the packet's size and the work to read it do not grow with the race. Until review of PR #198 the room kept every tape part in memory and sent them all, and the client stepped through every tick so far in one call, about 0.4 s for a 12,800-tick race on an M-series Mac. The rooms protocol is 3 since then. Once the rider's controls arrive the bike is theirs again. When the hold runs out the seat leaves as if quit, and the pilot rides it home. A claim compares the hold's deadline with the room's clock itself, so a rider who comes back after the hold ran out does not get the bike even when the hold's timer has not woken yet. A claim with `rejoin=no`, which "Back to the bar" sends, gives a held seat up at once with `SEAT_QUIT`. Nobody else sees any of this: a held rider looks the same as one riding.

The browser side has four screens in `apps/web/src/components/screens/rejoin.tsx`. A seat lost while riding moves the race to the `signalLost` stage, which shows the hold counting down and reloads `/?room=<id>&rejoin=try` as soon as the room's status route answers. `readRoomPreview` keeps 2 s of the hold for reopening the seat and fits two tries into the rest, at most one every 6 s: each request has two thirds of its turn to answer and the wait before the next try is the last third. A 30 s hold gives 4 s and 2 s, and the shortest hold a room allows, 5 s, gives 1 s and 0.5 s, so one hung request cannot use up the hold. Reopening the link goes straight in too, and a race that arrives with `awayMs` shows `Getting you back on` in place of the bar and skips the door moment. A rejoined race shows `Your bike is up` with the rider's place for 3 s. A `rejoin=try` arrival that lands in the lobby shows `We kept your bike`. Closing the tab sends the stats report and keeps the seat held; only the pause menu's quit leaves it.

A room keeps no state outside memory, so a restart or a deploy ends its race and forgets its lobby: members, host and rules. So does a Durable Object evicted while every rider is on results. The riders' tabs reconnect to the same room ID and find a new lobby with the default rules. The walkable lobby will replace the 2D room view and read the same lobby message. Rider position stays out of it, as its own stream. A rider in the lobby sends a `walk` command at most 8 times a second, only when it changed, and an `act` command for a greet or a shove. The host relays each to everyone else who is present and not racing, and keeps each member's last walk so someone who arrives later sees where everyone stands. It holds no rules about them: a walk is what the sender says it is, and nothing in a race reads it. `start` forgets the walks.

The room's tunable values live in one typed config, `launchRoomConfig` in `apps/rooms/src/room-config.ts`, and nowhere else. `RoomConfigSchema` gives each a minimum and a maximum, and `apps/rooms/test/room-config.test.ts` fails a change that leaves them. It also refuses two pairs that only make sense in order: an away listing shorter than the race hold, and a public `publicGatherSeconds` longer than `publicStartSeconds`, which would make the lobby's earliest start later than its latest. It holds the seat hold after a drop (`seatHoldSeconds`, 30 s), the custom lobby's start after the first rider readies (`customStartSeconds`, 60 s), the public lobby's start (`publicStartSeconds`, 45 s, or `publicGatherSeconds`, 10 s, once everyone is ready), the queue's wait (`queueWaitSeconds`, 30 s), how long an away member stays listed (`awaySeconds`, 120 s), the load deadline (`loadDeadlineSeconds`, 20 s), the public race (`publicRace`: tier 2, the starting bike, normal traffic and police, and the roads the server picks from) and the race limit (`raceLimitSeconds`, 6 minutes). A change goes through a pull request and deploys with the Worker. The host reads the config through its ports, and each race freezes its own values into its manifest (`loadingDeadlineMs`, `reconnect.holdSeconds`, `raceLimitSeconds`, and the rule), so a client builds the race the room runs. Values that change the netcode or the simulation rather than the feel stay in code: 60 ticks a second, eight seats, a field of ten, the ping and silence timings, tape and log sizes, and the name limit.

A public lobby is the same lobby under a `pub-` room ID. `roomKindOf` in `apps/rooms/src/room-id.ts` reads the kind from the ID, and the room fixes it when it opens. It has no host, the server picks its road for each race from the config's public roads with `publicRuleFor`, and its timer starts when the first rider arrives: `publicStartSeconds`, or `publicGatherSeconds` if everyone is ready, so riders still loading the page get in. `Find a race` reaches one through the queue. `createQueue` in `apps/rooms/src/queue.ts` keeps first-come tickets on one `QueueServer` Durable Object and forms a lobby the moment eight riders wait or the oldest has waited `queueWaitSeconds`. It sends each rider the new room ID and closes their ticket. A ticket runs the seat's own heartbeat from `apps/rooms/src/heartbeat.ts`, `createPings` in the browser and `createPongs` and `watchForSilence` in the room: the queue drops a ticket it has heard nothing from for 10 s and tells the rest the new count, and the browser gives up on a queue that leaves its pings unanswered for 5 s and opens a new ticket. Each ticket carries the browser's member key, so one browser holds at most one ticket. A second tab takes the first tab's ticket and its place in line, and the first tab reads `You opened the queue in another tab.` A rider who leaves the queue loses their place, and a deploy empties the queue, so a waiting screen reconnects and reads Reload. Friends queueing as a party is not built.

Each room runs a 60 Hz race and writes a tape, on an account shared with another product, so the `Rooms` Worker limits how fast one client can take seats, open rooms and enter the queue. `gateRules` in `apps/rooms/src/gate.ts` is the one table of limits, each a Workers Rate Limiting binding counted over 60 s: 60 seats, 6 room opens and 20 queue tickets per client. `route` in `apps/rooms/src/route.ts` checks a seat or queue ticket before it reaches any Durable Object, so a flood never queues on the one `QueueServer`. `RaceServer` checks the open gate when a seat arrives in a room with nobody present, because only the room knows that. A refused client gets a socket that carries one `refused` message with the reason `limited` and closes, and the room or queue screen reads "Too many riders joined from your network. Try again in a minute." with Back. A client is the address in `CF-Connecting-IP`, which Cloudflare sets to whoever connected to its edge, and an IPv6 client is its /64, because one machine can take any address in its /64. Everyone behind one address shares its limits, so a household or a café counts as one client: two full rooms of eight on one connection, each rider rejoining twice in a minute, is 48 seats. A request with no edge address or a loopback one comes from a local run and is not limited, so `pnpm rooms:load` still opens hundreds of seats against workerd. The limits are approximate and counted separately in each Cloudflare location, and a refusal writes no log line, because a burst would turn each refusal into a billed log event.

A room link is `/?room=<id>`, and `Race friends` on the bar makes one with a new ID. A guest name is the only identity a player sees. The browser keeps it in `rushline.guestName.v1` and sends it as a query parameter on the seat URL, and `cleanGuestName` in `apps/rooms/src/messages.ts` is what the server stores. Someone arriving by link first reads the room's rules through `readRoomPreview`, then chooses Join. The `started` message carries the humans' names in rider order, and `byGuest` in `packages/career/src/room-race.ts` maps them onto the race log's rows, because a seat that is not rider 0 reads rider 0 under a rival's slot. Results label every rider without a guest name as AI.
