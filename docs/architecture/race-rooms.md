# Race rooms

A race room runs one race for up to eight humans. The room is the authority: it steps the race at 60 Hz and every client follows its ticks. `packages/sim` holds the room's rules, so the same code runs in a Durable Object, in the browser and in Node tests. Ticks are the only clock. Nothing in `room*.ts` reads the time.

`roomSetup(setup, humans)` seats one to eight different racers as humans, rides every other racer on the pilot and turns pacing off, because pacing pulls the field toward the player and a room has no single player.

`createRoom(setup, pilotFromTick)` in `room.ts` keeps one seat per human. `accept(rider, firstTick, controls)` stores a client's controls by tick and reports how many were accepted, late or more than `INPUT_HORIZON` (90) ticks ahead. The first control for a tick wins, so a client can resend its recent controls in every packet. `step()` builds the tick's frame from the seats and steps the race. A seat whose control arrived rides it, and a seat the pilot was riding takes the bike back on that tick. A silent seat holds its last steer and pedals without punch or kick for `HOLD_TICKS` (6), then coasts, then goes to the pilot after `AUTO_AFTER_TICKS` (30). From `pilotFromTick` the pilot rides every seat and no control takes a bike back, so a tab left open cannot hold a room and its bill forever, and the client predicts that handover. The room and the client take that tick from the manifest's `raceLimitSeconds`, six minutes at launch. `leave(rider)` hands the seat to the pilot on the next tick and ignores its later controls. Every `HASH_EVERY` (60) ticks the room hashes its race.

The server starts a race, never a seat: no packet can start one. When the lobby's 60 s run out, the host freezes one `RaceManifest` (`apps/rooms/src/manifest.ts`) for that race: protocol, build, content and track hashes, seed, tick rate, the rule (bike, road and tier), the roster with AI labelled, each rider's bike, the loading deadline and how many seconds the room holds a dropped rider's bike (`reconnect.holdSeconds`). `started` gives every seat that manifest and its rider. Each seat builds the race from it and sends a ready packet carrying its setup digest. A digest that differs from the room's gets `refused` with `content` and loses its seat. When the last seat is ready, or leaves, or 20 s pass, the host sends `countdown` with one start moment on the room's own clock and steps from then. The lobby message and every pong carry the room's time, and each client keeps the earliest arrival it has seen against that time, so it knows when news from the room's start moment reaches it. Riders on different links count down to the same moment.

The rooms Worker writes no invocation logs, because each incoming WebSocket message would be a log event, and a log event costs about 80 times its message's share of a billed request. `host.ts` writes a few JSON lines per race through `ports.note` instead, typed by `RoomNoteSchema` in `apps/rooms/src/notes.ts`. A room ID comes from the invite link and can run several races, so each race gets its own random race ID from `ports.raceId`, and every note, the `started` message and `RoomReport` carry it. No note holds a guest name or the room ID. `started` names the build, the road, the seed and the setup digest. `ended` sums up every rider who started, including those who left: where they connected from, how long they took to load, what the room saw of their controls and crashes, and whether their browser's stats arrived. It also counts the gaps between the room's wakes. `pnpm rooms:read` turns those lines into a report per race.

Each browser also sends the room a race stats packet every 15 s while it rides and when its rider finishes, crashes out, leaves or closes the tab. `host.ts` keeps the latest and logs it once, when the seat's socket closes or the race ends, so a socket that dies still leaves a report. The `left` note carries the socket's close code. It counts how far corrections moved the rider's own bike and why, the rider's crashes and how many first appeared in a correction, the clock's held and doubled steps, the round trip and the slowest frame, because prod has no other record of what a rider's browser saw.

`host.ts` also saves each race's tick packets to the `RaceTapes` R2 bucket through `ports.saveTape`, so `pnpm rooms:replay` can run a race again and compare its hashes. [observability.md](../observability.md#race-tapes) has the format and the rules.

`room-wire.ts` holds the two packets. A client sends an input packet: a kind byte of 1, the first tick as a little-endian u32, a count from 1 to 8, then four bytes per control, 6 + 4n bytes in all. The room sends a tick packet: a kind byte of 2, the tick, a count of handovers with a rider byte and a controller byte each, a count of controls with a rider byte and four control bytes each, and a flag byte that says whether a 16-byte hash follows. The readers fill reused records and return `false` for any packet that is cut short, has bytes left over, carries a steer byte of 128, names a third controller or names a rider twice.

`createRoomClient(setup, me)` in `room-client.ts` holds two races. `confirmed` steps only on the room's frames, in order. `predicted` runs ahead and is the race the browser draws. `predict(control)` steps the predicted race one tick with this rider's control and a guess for everyone else: each remote human holds their last confirmed control without buttons, and this rider takes the bike back from the pilot. It refuses to run more than `MOST_LEAD_TICKS` (90, the same as `INPUT_HORIZON`) ahead of the confirmed race. `confirm(frame, hash)` steps the confirmed race and compares the room's frame with the guess for that tick. When they differ it writes the confirmed race to a snapshot, restores it into the predicted race in place, so the renderer keeps the same objects, and steps forward again to the tick it had reached. It counts rollbacks, resimulated ticks and desyncs, a desync being a room hash that differs from the confirmed race's. `writeInputs(view)` writes one packet of up to eight controls that starts with the oldest control not yet sent and fills the rest with recent unconfirmed ones, and returns 0 once every predicted control has gone out, so a client that predicted many ticks at once calls it until it returns 0.

The client hears this rider's own events from the predicted race as it steps, and every other event from the confirmed race; `takeEvents()` hands over everything heard since the last call, a burst of the room's ticks included. `takeFights()` hands over each `attack` and `hit` that either race steps through, with the tick it happened on: the predicted race runs this rider's strikes and every AI rider's strikes on time, and the confirmed race brings the other humans' strikes and every hit a burst copies in without stepping it. A local race hands over its own fights the same way. The cast keeps one strike track per rider in `rider-fight.ts`. It ages every track once per step and takes new fights at the start of every draw, after any steps that frame ran, so a frame drawn between ticks, or after several, still draws each strike and hit with the race it shows. A rider can start one strike per cooldown, so a strike heard again inside that cooldown, from the other race or a rollback, is the same strike and plays once. A strike starts at elapsed min(N, contact), where N is the drawn tick minus the tick the strike started and contact is its ticks to the hit, so a strike heard late appears at its contact pose on the frame its shove lands rather than after it. A strike heard while another is playing waits and starts when that one ends. A `hit` cancels the strike its target was running at the hit's tick, playing or waiting, as the sim does, and leaves strikes that start later. The flinch starts in full on the frame the drawn race first shows a strike landing on the rider. A hit within one stun of the last one shown is the same hit moved by a correction and doesn't start the flinch again. A bump sets `lastHitTick` too but never makes a rider flinch.

The client reconciles every hit the predicted race shows with the room, matched by striker, target and attack within 15 ticks rather than by the exact tick. A shown hit the confirmed race lands is settled. A shown hit that a rollback no longer produces, or that the confirmed race has passed by 15 ticks without landing, is taken back: `takeFights()` hands over a `RefusedHit`, and the cast ends the target's flinch if it is still running for that hit and no other rider's hit on them is showing. A refused hit keeps its record until the room passes it, so a later rollback that finds it again shows it again without a second sound. The attacker hears each of their own hits once, when it first shows: in the predicted race as it steps, in a rollback that finds a hit the guess missed, or in the confirmed race when the prediction never showed it. A hit that a correction moves, because the room started the strike a few ticks later than the guess, is the same hit and is not heard again. A refused hit was already heard; the sound is not taken back.

`packages/sim/src/room.test.ts` races clients over simulated links in ticks, each with its own latency, jitter and lead, through the encoded packets. Two riders racing twenty seconds end on the room's race hash for hash, and each one's predicted race equals the room's at the tick it stopped predicting. A rider who goes silent is handed to the pilot exactly 31 ticks after the last tick it was heard for and takes the bike back when its controls arrive again. A rider whose lead is shorter than its latency loses its controls to the pilot and still agrees with the room. A netcode change is also judged in two real windows, with `pnpm rooms:fights` on its base and head builds and `pnpm rooms:compare` holding the head to the rule table in `tools/rooms/src/fight-compare.ts`, as the [netcode compare recipe](../../.claude/skills/verify-rushline/features/netcode-compare.md) says.

## The room on a Durable Object

`alchemy.run.ts` declares a second Worker, `Rooms`, from `apps/rooms/src/worker.ts`. It is separate from the site so that a merge that leaves the simulation alone does not restart a live race, and so the room's bundle carries no React. `Rooms` answers `/rooms/:roomId...` by looking up the `RaceServer` Durable Object named by that room ID and forwarding the request. A malformed ID answers 404. `race-server.ts` serves the contract in `contract.ts`, an Effect `HttpApi`: `GET /rooms/:roomId` returns the room's status as JSON, and `GET /rooms/:roomId/seat` upgrades to a WebSocket, or answers 426 to any other request.

Cloudflare creates a Durable Object near the colo of the first request that reaches its name, keeps it there for life, and reads a location hint only on that first request. The Worker passes no hint, so a room runs near the colo of the first player who opens it. A browser's preview and its seat enter Cloudflare at the same colo, so whichever comes first places the room the same way. That colo is also where the rider's socket enters, so a room near it is as near as that rider's network allows. Production notes from November 5 to 8, 2026, read whole, show what this gives riders in India, which has no Durable Object colo. All 32 races with a logged `roomColo` ran in a colo that none of their seats entered through. Races whose riders all entered at DEL ran in SIN 22 times, with a median round trip of 104 ms, and in HKG 8 times, with a median of 132 ms, while those riders' TCP time to the edge had a median of 5 ms. On November 9, 2026, [where.durableobjects.live](https://where.durableobjects.live/colo/DEL) reported that DEL hosts no Durable Objects and places them in SIN 58% of the time at 88 ms and in HKG 42% of the time at 135 ms, and that BOM places every one in SIN. No hint does better. `apac` spreads objects over SIN, HKG, NRT, KIX and ICN. `apac-se` resolves from one point whatever the origin, SIN 65% and HKG 35%, so it would move some Mumbai rooms to HKG. A hint chosen from the rider's country is wrong for Indian networks that enter Cloudflare abroad: a Jio rider entering at NRT gets an NRT room today, and the hint would pull it to SIN. The riders who saw 260 to 604 ms entered at NRT, SIN or MRS with 114 to 176 ms of TCP time to the edge, a leg that placement cannot shorten. One room ran in IAD for two riders who entered at SIN, and the notes do not record which request created it. The lobby shows every member's round trip, so riders in a far room see it before they race. To check where rooms ran after a deploy, read the next races with `pnpm rooms:read` and compare each race's `roomColo` with its seats' colos.

`host.ts` is the room's plain core, given a clock, a timer and a seed as ports, so tests drive it without a Worker. A seat joins in the waiting phase and every seat hears a `lobby` message. A seat states its build and wire protocol when it connects, and anything other than the room's own gets `refused` with `build` before it sits, which the room screen shows as a reload. The room's build is `RUSHLINE_BUILD`. `alchemy.run.ts` reads it once with `roomsBuild` from `apps/rooms/src/build-id.ts`, through Alchemy's config, which also holds `.env` and `--env-file` values that never reach `process.env`. It hands that one value to the `Rooms` Worker, pinned with `pinRoomsBuild` so the Worker and both Durable Objects read it at deploy and again when they run, and to the game's build as `VITE_ROOMS_BUILD`, which Alchemy inlines into the client. A deploy with no build, or with `local`, fails before it ships, and a Durable Object whose Worker has no build stops at init. Only `alchemy dev` runs as `local`. `rv` builds the game with plain Vite for those rooms, so it sets `VITE_ROOMS_BUILD` to `local` itself. `scripts/rooms-build.test.ts` plans the stack and checks that the game and the rooms get the same build. Tools read the build from the status route. A room sends a ninth seat `refused` with `full`, then closes it. The message carries the reason because a client cannot rely on seeing the close finish. A member who arrives during a race waits in the lobby for the next one. The host gives out riders in the order it holds the seats, which after a hibernation is the order the runtime returns them, so no rule may read a meaning into a rider number. It builds the race with `seatedRace(seed, seats, rule)` from `packages/career/src/room-race.ts`, which is the career's practice setup under `launchRules` with `roomSetup` applied, every human on one spec, every AI racer on the same bike with its own skill, and the humans on the front of the grid, and tells each seat `started` with the manifest and its rider.

`hostAfter` in `apps/rooms/src/host-phase.ts` decides every step of a room's race, and `createHost` changes its state nowhere else. The room is `waiting` with no race, `loading` from the moment `start` builds the race until the last seat is ready or has left or the 20 s deadline passes, `countdown` for the 3 s to the start moment, `racing` from the first wake, and `waiting` again the moment `room.over()` says so. A seat is still loading while it is `here` and its `readyMs` is null, and nothing else records that, so a rider whose link dropped during loading does not hold the countdown. A race's end is a move, not a phase: the room reads `waiting` at once, and each finished rider's 5 s to report belongs to that seat. The state names its race, so the load deadline of a race that has already ended cannot start the next race's countdown. Only a move builds a state, and a wake builds none. The status route's `hostPhase` says which of the four the room is in. Its `phase` stays `waiting` or `racing`, reading `racing` from `loading` on, because a tab on the previous deploy decodes the route with `decodeRoomPreview`, which fails on any other value and retries.

`roleAfter` in `apps/rooms/src/socket-role.ts` decides what each connected socket is, and `createHost` changes a role nowhere else. The host keeps one record per socket in `present`: its member, its last lobby walk and its role. A socket is `lobby` from the moment it sits, `seated` with its race and seat from the moment the race is built, and `reporting` with its seat from the race's end until its 5 s grace runs out. The host then writes the report, closes the socket with 1000 and puts it back in `lobby` until its close arrives. Only `lobby` sockets hear lobby messages and walks, only `seated` sockets hear race messages and ticks, and a `reporting` socket's messages only fill in its report, so they do not count as hearing from it. A socket that leaves has no role, and its member reads away in the lobby, unless it closed with `SEAT_LEFT` (4410), which the lobby's Leave button sends, and so does a room whose game failed to boot (`seatClosing` in `apps/web/src/room-handoff.ts`): then the member leaves the lobby at once, so the others see the spot free and a custom room's host passes on. Every other close keeps the member away, including the 1000 a seat sends after a race and the 1001 a reload or a closed tab sends. A seated socket that closes with `SEAT_LEFT` or `SEAT_QUIT` (4411, the pause menu's quit) leaves its seat: the rider goes to the pilot and the host writes the `left` line and the report. Any other close holds the seat for `seatHoldSeconds` (see below) and writes a `held` line and the report. Leaving while reporting writes the report. A member who arrives on a new socket replaces the old one, which hears `refused` with `replaced` and leaves the same way, except that the member is not away and keeps its ready flag, colour and walk. Each `Seat` in the race's `riders` outlives its socket, so `ended` sums up every rider who started. The status route's `roles` lists each socket's role in the order of `names`. The phase says what the race is doing and the role says what each socket is, so neither machine reads the other's state.

The tick loop is an absolute-deadline `setTimeout` chain: tick N is due N/60 s after the start, a wake steps what is due and at most four ticks, and a room still behind wakes again at once. The status counts wakes, wakes that had to catch up and the worst backlog, because `Date.now()` does not advance while a Durable Object runs and these are the only clock facts a room can report. Each tick goes to every seat as the tick packet. Control messages from the room are JSON text (`messages.ts`). A client sends binary packets, input, ping, ready and race stats, in `room-wire.ts`, and JSON text lobby commands, `ready`, `choose`, `walk` and `act`, in `apps/rooms/src/messages.ts`. A ping gets a pong with the room's tick, which is how a client sets its lead. Each ping also carries the round trip its browser measured on the last pong, the time from sending a ping to hearing the room's answer, so it covers the rider's link to Cloudflare and Cloudflare's path to the room. The room does not measure it. It reads the ping's two bytes, whole ms from 0 to 65534, or 65535 for none, and lists each member's latest value in the lobby message, null until one arrives and while the member is away. A browser that sends a false number changes only what the others read about that browser, and nothing but the lobby reads it. The host sends the lobby again on its one-second silence sweep when a value changed, never on the ping itself. So a new measurement reaches the other riders after the browser's next ping, up to 500 ms later, the trip to the room, up to 1 s until the sweep, and the trip back. A race ends when `room.over()` says every seat has left or finished, been busted or wrecked. Every seat then hears `ended` and closes with 1000, and the room waits for a new race. A seat that closes mid-race is held, and the pilot rides it once its controls stop. A lost link closes nothing, so every client pings from the moment its socket opens, and the host drops any seat it has heard nothing from for 10 s, in the lobby or the race, exactly as if it had closed. Without this the pilots rode a race whose riders had all gone to the finish, and no `left` line was written.

A browser joins with `/?room=<room id>`. `openRoomSeat` in `apps/rooms/src/seat-client.ts` owns the socket and the room clock, pings through `createPings` in `apps/rooms/src/heartbeat.ts`, and `pnpm rooms:race` uses the same client. `seatAfter` in the same file is the only thing that changes a seat's phase. A loading, racing or ended seat holds its race in the phase value, and `ended`, `left`, `lost` and `refused` are final. When the race starts, `createRoomRun` makes a `RaceRun` whose `view` is the predicted race, and the game draws it like any other race. `stage.me` names the local rider, so the camera, the HUD and the race log follow that rider instead of rider 0. The race log reads the confirmed race, so a guess the room later corrects cannot settle a result. It settles on the rider's own state in that race, finished, busted or wrecked, so a log that starts after a rejoin still settles a bust or a finish that happened while the bike was held. `createRoomClock` in `packages/sim/src/room-clock.ts` leads the room's tick by the typical round trip (the median of the last ten pings), plus how late ticks arrive, plus two ticks. Lateness is the spread between the earliest tick heard in the last 2 s and the 90th-percentile latest, so a few seconds of slower delivery, or ticks heard in bursts while the race loads, stop counting 2 s after they end. Until November 6, 2026 a tracker measured lateness against the clock's long-lived arrival line and fell 3 ms a second: a loading stall at the start left the lead 13 to 15 ticks too high 40 s into a production race, and rooms held each control 150 to 290 ms. A ping slower than that raises the lead to cover it at once, and so does a tick the room ran without this rider's control (`lostControls()` on the room client), by two ticks for each run of controls sent before the last raise. The raise is a round trip, so a typical round trip that climbs to meet it uses it up rather than adding to it, and it fades to nothing over 2 s, so a lag spike costs a few seconds of lateness rather than fifteen. A button press the room rode none of the ticks of is queued and sent again: from the next tick the client predicts, it holds that button until its own race starts the strike, or for at most a second, then moves to the next missed press. So a press made as the link slows still lands, two presses a stall swallowed both land, and the presser may see their own strike twice. The stats line counts these presses as `retriedPresses`, so a real race shows how often that happens. Only controls predicted before a confirm count as lost for the lead, so ticks a paused browser never predicted go through the catch-up path instead. It takes the latest tick heard as a floor rather than a reset, so one late packet moves nothing. Off target by two ticks or more it eases, adding a step every sixth frame or holding one every third, and it steps at once only when the controls it would send would arrive too late. A lead 12 ticks or more past what the latest round trip needs shows the lag tag.

A lobby outlives its races. `createLobby` in `apps/rooms/src/lobby.ts` holds the members, the rule, who is ready and the start timer, as plain data with no sockets, so any screen can draw it. Each member has a stable ID, and the browser's `rushline.memberKey.v1` in localStorage puts it back on the same member after a reload, a dropped link or a closed tab, when the rider opens the link again in any tab of that browser. A browser that refuses storage keeps one key for the life of the page, so a seat the page reopens is still the same member. Every tab of one browser is the same member, so a second tab that joins takes the seat and the first reads `You opened this lobby in another tab.` A member whose link drops stays listed as away for 2 minutes; one who pressed Leave is not listed, and comes back as a new member. The host is the oldest member who is here, so the host's seat passes on and comes back without anyone transferring it. The member list shows each rider's round trip to the room in ms, as that rider's browser measured it, plain under 150, yellow under 250 and red from 250. A line under the list says that the number is the round trip to this room, so nobody reads it as a ping to the nearest Cloudflare edge. Only the host can choose the road, tier, traffic and police, and a change clears everyone's ready. The race starts the moment everyone here is ready, or a minute after the first rider readied, and takes everyone here. A rider who sits down during a race waits in the lobby and rides the next one.

`seatedRace` scales traffic and police from the tier's own numbers in `difficulties`, so that table stays the only source of the values. Bikes are not a choice: every racer rides `rule.bikeId`.

After a race each rider's socket closes, because the client closes on `ended` and the room closes any left after 5 s, the time it allows for a late report. `Back to the lobby` on results reloads `/?room=<id>`, and the same member sits down again. A rider who loses the room mid-race gets their bike back. Each seat in a race is `here`, `held` since a moment, or `left` at a tick (`SeatPresence` in `apps/rooms/src/race-seat.ts`). A seat whose socket drops is held for the room config's `seatHoldSeconds`, 30 s at launch; the lobby message and the manifest carry it to the browser. The pilot rides the held bike as it rides any silent seat. When the same member sits down again while the seat is held, the host rejoins them: the socket goes straight to `seated` with `started`, whose `awayMs` says how long they were gone, then one catch-up packet (kind 8 in `room-wire.ts`) for each tape part so far, then `countdown` if the race has a start moment, and a `rejoined` line. `RoomClient.catchUp` steps the confirmed race through each part with its hash checks and no events, and the predicted race restarts from it, so the rider draws the room's race at the room's tick. A 12,800-tick race replays in about 0.4 s on an M-series Mac. Once the rider's controls arrive the bike is theirs again. When the hold runs out the seat leaves as if quit, and the pilot rides it home. A claim compares the hold's deadline with the room's clock itself, so a rider who comes back after the hold ran out does not get the bike even when the hold's timer has not woken yet. A claim with `rejoin=no`, which "Back to the bar" sends, gives a held seat up at once with `SEAT_QUIT`. Nobody else sees any of this: a held rider looks the same as one riding.

The browser side has four screens in `apps/web/src/components/screens/rejoin.tsx`. A seat lost while riding moves the race to the `signalLost` stage, which shows the hold counting down and reloads `/?room=<id>&rejoin=try` as soon as the room's status route answers. `readRoomPreview` keeps 2 s of the hold for reopening the seat and fits two tries into the rest, at most one every 6 s: each request has two thirds of its turn to answer and the wait before the next try is the last third. A 30 s hold gives 4 s and 2 s, and the shortest hold a room allows, 5 s, gives 1 s and 0.5 s, so one hung request cannot use up the hold. Reopening the link goes straight in too, and a race that arrives with `awayMs` shows `Getting you back on` in place of the bar and skips the door moment. A rejoined race shows `Your bike is up` with the rider's place for 3 s. A `rejoin=try` arrival that lands in the lobby shows `We kept your bike`. Closing the tab sends the stats report and keeps the seat held; only the pause menu's quit leaves it.

A room keeps one record outside memory, `RoomMemory` in `apps/rooms/src/room-memory.ts`, in its Durable Object's storage under the key `room`: the lobby's members (id, key, name and colour), its rule and join counter, and the race, which is its manifest, its seats and a checkpoint of the race's tick, its snapshot bytes and the tape's unsaved tail. The host writes it through `ports.remember` when a member joins or leaves, when the host changes the rule, when a race is built, at the start moment and every `CHECKPOINT_TICKS` (300, 5 s) while racing, and when the race ends. It deletes it when the lobby is empty. The snapshot is the race's own codec, 17.5 KB for a ten-rider race, and the tail is at most one part, so a write is one row of under 170 KB, 72 rows in a six-minute race, and costs 0.11 ms of the tick that writes it. `race-server.ts` queues the writes on a sliding queue of one, so a burst of lobby changes writes once, and notes `memoryLost` if a write fails.

When the Durable Object restarts, every socket dies without a close frame, the browsers read 1006 and the race screen shows `Signal lost`. The new instance reads the memory in its constructor, before any request, and `createHost` restores from it. The lobby's members come back away, so the same keys map to the same ids, colours and host order, and a race with a checkpoint written inside the last `seatHoldSeconds` comes back at its checkpoint tick: `restoreRace` builds the race from the manifest's seed, rule and seats, restores the snapshot into it, marks the seats that had left, seeds the tape writer with the saved tail and part count so the tape in R2 stays one whole race, and the host holds every other seat for the full hold, re-bases the start moment so the race goes on from the checkpoint with no catch-up burst, and writes a `restored` line. The riders' tabs reload with `rejoin=try` within a few seconds and take the normal rejoin path: `started` with `awayMs`, then one race snapshot packet (kind 9 in `room-wire.ts`), which `RoomClient.catchUpFrom` restores into the confirmed race, then the tape since the checkpoint, then `countdown`. Everyone loses at most five seconds of racing and no result. A deploy that changes the snapshot format or the race's content makes the restore refuse the snapshot, so a sim change never runs on a stale state.

A race the memory holds with no checkpoint, one whose checkpoint is older than the hold, or one the new build refuses, is lost: the host writes a `lost` line with the reason, keeps the lobby, and names the race in the lobby message's `lostRace` until the next race starts, so a `rejoin=try` arrival reads `Room restarted. That race is gone` instead of `We kept your bike`. That is the cancel path section 12 of the design doc allows, and it is now the exception. Before November 10, 2026 every restart took this path with the wrong card. The choice was made on these facts: Cloudflare terminates every WebSocket when an object shuts down, and the default code update strategy is `deferred` with a 300 s `max_delay`, so a racing room, which never hibernates, resets five minutes after any deploy of the Rooms Worker if its race is still running, and every deploy ships a new Rooms Worker because `RUSHLINE_BUILD` changes. Production logs for November 3 to 9 show 84 races, none cut, four with a deploy inside them that all ended within five minutes of it, and 16 deploys on November 8 at a median gap of 9 minutes. A restart therefore cuts a race that outlives the deferral, a lobby that someone sits in for five minutes after a deploy, and any race hit by an eviction, a runtime update or a crash. Deferring updates longer was rejected: Alchemy 2.0.0-beta.83 has no `code_update_strategy`, the field is write-only and best effort, and a rider who reloads during a deferral gets the new build and is refused by a room on the old one.

The walkable lobby will replace the 2D room view and read the same lobby message. Rider position stays out of it, as its own stream. A rider in the lobby sends a `walk` command at most 8 times a second, only when it changed, and an `act` command for a greet or a shove. The host relays each to everyone else who is present and not racing, and keeps each member's last walk so someone who arrives later sees where everyone stands. It holds no rules about them: a walk is what the sender says it is, and nothing in a race reads it. `start` forgets the walks.

The room's tunable values live in one typed config, `launchRoomConfig` in `apps/rooms/src/room-config.ts`, and nowhere else. `RoomConfigSchema` gives each a minimum and a maximum, and `apps/rooms/test/room-config.test.ts` fails a change that leaves them. It also refuses two pairs that only make sense in order: an away listing shorter than the race hold, and a public `publicGatherSeconds` longer than `publicStartSeconds`, which would make the lobby's earliest start later than its latest. It holds the seat hold after a drop (`seatHoldSeconds`, 30 s), the custom lobby's start after the first rider readies (`customStartSeconds`, 60 s), the public lobby's start (`publicStartSeconds`, 45 s, or `publicGatherSeconds`, 10 s, once everyone is ready), the queue's wait (`queueWaitSeconds`, 30 s), how long an away member stays listed (`awaySeconds`, 120 s), the load deadline (`loadDeadlineSeconds`, 20 s), the public race (`publicRace`: tier 2, the starting bike, normal traffic and police, and the roads the server picks from) and the race limit (`raceLimitSeconds`, 6 minutes). A change goes through a pull request and deploys with the Worker. The host reads the config through its ports, and each race freezes its own values into its manifest (`loadingDeadlineMs`, `reconnect.holdSeconds`, `raceLimitSeconds`, and the rule), so a client builds the race the room runs. Values that change the netcode or the simulation rather than the feel stay in code: 60 ticks a second, eight seats, a field of ten, the ping and silence timings, tape and log sizes, and the name limit.

A public lobby is the same lobby under a `pub-` room ID. `roomKindOf` in `apps/rooms/src/room-id.ts` reads the kind from the ID, and the room fixes it when it opens. It has no host, the server picks its road for each race from the config's public roads with `publicRuleFor`, and its timer starts when the first rider arrives: `publicStartSeconds`, or `publicGatherSeconds` if everyone is ready, so riders still loading the page get in. `Find a race` reaches one through the queue. `createQueue` in `apps/rooms/src/queue.ts` keeps first-come tickets on one `QueueServer` Durable Object and forms a lobby the moment eight riders wait or the oldest has waited `queueWaitSeconds`. It sends each rider the new room ID and closes their ticket. A ticket runs the seat's own heartbeat from `apps/rooms/src/heartbeat.ts`, `createPings` in the browser and `createPongs` and `watchForSilence` in the room: the queue drops a ticket it has heard nothing from for 10 s and tells the rest the new count, and the browser gives up on a queue that leaves its pings unanswered for 5 s and opens a new ticket. Each ticket carries the browser's member key, so one browser holds at most one ticket. A second tab takes the first tab's ticket and its place in line, and the first tab reads `You opened the queue in another tab.` A rider who leaves the queue loses their place, and a deploy empties the queue, so a waiting screen reconnects and reads Reload. Friends queueing as a party is not built.

Each room runs a 60 Hz race and writes a tape, on an account shared with another product, so the `Rooms` Worker limits how fast one client can take seats, open rooms and enter the queue. `gateRules` in `apps/rooms/src/gate.ts` is the one table of limits, each a Workers Rate Limiting binding counted over 60 s: 60 seats, 6 room opens and 20 queue tickets per client. `route` in `apps/rooms/src/route.ts` checks a seat or queue ticket before it reaches any Durable Object, so a flood never queues on the one `QueueServer`. `RaceServer` checks the open gate when a seat arrives in a room with nobody present, because only the room knows that. A refused client gets a socket that carries one `refused` message with the reason `limited` and closes, and the room or queue screen reads "Too many riders joined from your network. Try again in a minute." with Back. A client is the address in `CF-Connecting-IP`, which Cloudflare sets to whoever connected to its edge, and an IPv6 client is its /64, because one machine can take any address in its /64. Everyone behind one address shares its limits, so a household or a café counts as one client: two full rooms of eight on one connection, each rider rejoining twice in a minute, is 48 seats. A request with no edge address or a loopback one comes from a local run and is not limited, so `pnpm rooms:load` still opens hundreds of seats against workerd. The limits are approximate and counted separately in each Cloudflare location, and a refusal writes no log line, because a burst would turn each refusal into a billed log event.

A room has one thread, and the 60 Hz tick loop shares it with every message its sockets send. `host.ts` spends each message against its socket's budget before it reads the message, which bounds the work the room does per message and stops a socket that keeps flooding. `socketAllowances` in `apps/rooms/src/socket-budget.ts` is the one table of budgets, a token bucket per class of message: `packet` for the binary input, ping, ready and stats packets up to `MOST_PACKET_BYTES` (129 bytes, the stats packet), `command` for lobby text up to `MOST_COMMAND_CHARS` (1024 characters), and `trace` for a browser trace up to `MOST_TRACE_CHARS`. A longer message is dropped unread. Each rate is twice what an honest client sends, and each burst is twice what it sends in the 10 s a link can stall before the room drops its seat. A browser sends at most one input packet for each tick it predicts and never predicts more than 90 ticks past the room, plus two pings a second, so packets get 124 a second and 1240 at once. A rider walks at most `WALKS_PER_SECOND` (8, in `packages/sim/src/lobby-walk.ts`), and the budget assumes no rider presses keys more than 10 times a second, which the client does not enforce, so commands get 36 a second and 360 at once. A rider sends a trace with each report every 15 s, and at most three close together, so traces get one each 7.5 s and 6 at once. `decodeLobbyCommand` does not parse text longer than a command. The room drops a message over its budget and counts it in `dropped`, which the status route and the `ended` note carry for the race. A socket that has dropped `CUT_AFTER_DROPS` (64) messages hears `refused` with `flooded` and closes with `SEAT_FLOODED` (4429). Its rider goes to the pilot and its member reads away. The room screen reads "The room closed this seat because it sent too many messages." with Leave, and a browser cut mid-race moves to the room with `rejoin=flooded` to read it, with no way back onto its bike. `pnpm rooms:race` and `pnpm rooms:load` fail when a room dropped any message.

The budget cannot bound what the runtime spends delivering each message, which the room pays even for a message it drops at once. On local workerd that is about 1.2 to 1.8 ms a message, and a socket's messages already in flight are still delivered after it closes. A burst of 8,000 pings sent at once had the socket cut at its 1,723rd message, then still delivered more than 6,000 to the closed socket, so the room fell 821 ticks behind and took 14.7 s to recover. The 1240-packet allowance alone, sent at once, can stall the room for about a second there. The cost in production is not measured. The room cannot hold back its own sends to a slow socket either, because Cloudflare's server WebSocket has no `bufferedAmount` to read.

A room link is `/?room=<id>`, and `Race friends` on the bar makes one with a new ID. A guest name is the only identity a player sees. The browser keeps it in `rushline.guestName.v1` and sends it as a query parameter on the seat URL, and `cleanGuestName` in `apps/rooms/src/messages.ts` is what the server stores. Someone arriving by link first reads the room's rules through `readRoomPreview`, then chooses Join. The `started` message carries the humans' names in rider order, and `byGuest` in `packages/career/src/room-race.ts` maps them onto the race log's rows, because a seat that is not rider 0 reads rider 0 under a rival's slot. Results label every rider without a guest name as AI.

## Transport

Each seat is one WebSocket. It runs over TCP from the browser to the Cloudflare edge where the rider enters, and Cloudflare carries it on to the room's Durable Object. The room sends a tick packet every 16.7 ms. TCP delivers bytes in order, so when one segment is lost, every later tick waits until the sender resends it. This is head-of-line blocking, and `pnpm rooms:lag` models it by holding every packet behind a lost one. How long the hold lasts depends on how the sender detects the loss and on its timers, such as RACK-TLP's probe timeout of about twice the smoothed round trip (RFC 8985), and on which leg lost the segment: the rider's leg to the edge or a leg inside Cloudflare's path. None of that was measured here. Sending each tick twice cannot help on TCP, because the copy waits behind the lost segment in the same stream.

On November 9, 2026, `pnpm rooms:trace --races` read every rider trace in the RaceTapes bucket: 62 riders in 35 races, 3 hours 2 minutes of racing between November 4 and 8. 56 riders entered Cloudflare at DEL, and where the notes name the room's colo, their rooms ran in SIN or HKG. 27 of the races were saved before November 6 in the older `rows` format, and each of their rider files was rewritten as one connection with the command in the race-room recipe before the read. Tick gaps of 50, 100, 150, 250 and 500 ms or more took 7.47%, 3.11%, 0.83%, 0.49% and 0.11% of all racing. Of the 3.11% in gaps of 100 ms or more, frozen browsers caused 0.02%, link gaps with a round-trip spike 0.79%, link gaps with a steady round trip 0.86% and link gaps with none measured 1.45%. A spike shows that a pong waited in the same stream, which any stall on the way down causes, so the round trip cannot single out retransmits. Link gaps of 100 ms or more took under 1% of the race for only 25 of the 62 riders. The median rider spent 1.35% of the race in them, the 90th percentile 10.99% and the worst rider 18.04%.

Two observations bear on whether these gaps are TCP retransmits. Each leaves hypotheses that these traces fit but cannot settle, and the cause split behind them is a heuristic (see [observability.md](../observability.md#race-traces)):

- Size. A one-off count over the same 42,241 trace rows found 5,139 with a worst gap of 90 to 109 ms and only 821 with a longer one. Gaps of 150 ms or more took 0.83% of racing against 3.11% for 100 ms or more. The five riders with the most gaps of 90 to 109 ms had mean round trips to the room of 113 to 164 ms, a median frame of 17 to 19 ms in those rows, and the same peak. The notes measure the whole round trip and the TCP leg from the browser to the edge, 5 to 8 ms for these five, but not the legs inside Cloudflare's path to the room. So the peak fits a fixed delay of about 100 ms on the way to the browser, such as a Wi-Fi radio that sleeps between its access point's 102.4 ms beacons, and it also fits loss recovery on an inner leg that takes about 100 ms for every rider, an assumption about timers and legs the traces cannot check. Racing one seat on a wired link beside one on Wi-Fi, from the same place, is evidence that could help separate the two: a radio's delay would show on the Wi-Fi seat only, while loss on Cloudflare's path could reach either seat, though separate flows can see different losses. A packet capture on a rider's machine would show whether the edge resends a segment at each gap.
- Direction. A lost segment on the way down holds only the way down. A one-off count over the `pnpm rooms:trace --dir` reports of the 27 races with two traced riders found that the room missed the gapped rider's controls in 87 of the 1,496 seconds that held a gap of 100 ms or more, and in 26 of the 87 that held a gap of 250 ms or more. Both counts are grouped by second, so they put the two in the same second, not at the same moment. The large gaps may have stalled both directions at once, which the same packet capture would show.

All 85 `ended` notes from November 2 to 9 counted every gap between the room's wakes at 20 ms or less, with no wake that had to catch up, so the room's tick loop sent its ticks on time.

Riders who entered far from home had more gap time of their own. The two who entered at MRS, 173 and 176 ms from the edge, in races 9faa8ad7 and be5cc9dc, spent 2.71% and 2.47% of the race in link gaps of 100 ms or more, against 0.00% and 0.16% for the DEL rider beside them, and the room missed their controls in one of those seconds. Most of their gaps were 100 to 149 ms. A third rider entered at MRS, 147 ms from the edge, and spent 18.04% of race f3e36bc9 in link gaps, and the room missed that rider's controls in 6 of the 20 seconds that held one.

The rooms stay on WebSocket. No unreliable transport reaches a Durable Object today, as the options below say, and long tick gaps are a small share of race time: gaps of 150 ms or more took 0.83% of all racing and under 1% of the race for 41 of the 62 riders, and gaps of 250 ms or more took under 1% for 51. Link gaps of 100 ms or more are more common, and most of their time sits in the peak near 100 ms: of the 3.11% of racing in them, gaps of 100 to 149 ms took 2.28 points. Whether that peak comes from lost segments, and whether another transport would shorten it, is untested, so the wired seat beside the Wi-Fi seat and a packet capture during a race come before any transport work. Reconsider when a later `pnpm rooms:trace --races` read shows tick gaps of 150 ms or more taking 1% of the race or more for most riders, with the `pnpm rooms:trace --dir` reports showing missed controls in few of those seconds. Reconsider too when many riders enter Cloudflare far from home, as the MRS riders did, or when a capture shows the edge resending at most gaps.

The options when that changes:

- WebTransport datagrams would let the room send each tick unreliably with the last few ticks attached, and keep the WebSocket for everything else. Browsers support them, but Workers cannot accept them. The workerd request for WebTransport (cloudflare/workerd#6451) was closed on March 28, 2026 with a maintainer's reply that it is not on the priority list, because the Workers runtime has no QUIC or HTTP/3 implementation. The Cloudflare changelog to November 9, 2026 announces none.
- A WebRTC data channel set to `ordered: false` and `maxRetransmits: 0` drops a late tick instead of holding the ticks after it. Cloudflare Realtime's SFU has carried data channels with those settings since August 13, 2026, but only browsers and native apps hold the channels, so a Durable Object cannot be the other end. The room would need a WebRTC peer on a server outside Durable Objects, such as a Container or a VM, a second connection per seat beside the WebSocket, ICE and TURN, and a second service to deploy and pay for.
- A second WebSocket per seat carrying the same ticks would let one socket's retransmit hide behind the other, for twice the downstream traffic. It does nothing for a shared or two-way stall, and it is untested.
