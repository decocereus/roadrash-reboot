# Race setup and snapshots

## Race setup and cast

`createRace` takes a `RaceSetup`: the track and seed, the player (a profile, a bike, and the weapon and hits they carry in), the rivals for this race (each a profile and a bike, with an optional `grudge` naming a rider by `RiderId` and an optional `aggressionBoost` toward the player), the police settings (count, pursuit top speed, sight range, beat, fine, the officer's profile, and the bike they ride), the pacing rule (`leadEase` and `catchUp`), and the grid order. `rosterOf(setup)` gives every rider a `RiderId` once, the player first as `PLAYER_RIDER`, then the rivals in the order given, then the police. `RaceState.riders` holds them in that order for the whole race, and `riderOf(list, id)` reads a rider's entry from `riders` or from any list kept one per rider without allocating. Every reference from one rider to another, such as a grudge, a cop's chase, `lastHitBy` and the riders an event names, is a `RiderId`, and a missing one is `null`. The career keys its race log by the same ids. With `playerLast` the rivals fill the grid one to a lane across the whole road, rows 7 m apart, and the player has a row to themselves behind them. With `playerFirst` the player is on the front row. On a road with one lane each way the grid is two abreast and straddles the centre line; with two lanes each way it is four abreast. The player's row starts in the same-way lane next to the centre line.

`validateSetup(setup)` returns a list of typed problems: more rivals than `MOST_RACERS` (15) leaves room for beside the player, a rival listed twice, an entrant with no bike, or a grudge against the rival itself or against a rider who is not racing. `createRace` calls it and throws one error carrying every message, so a caller at a boundary validates first.

`defaultSetup(track, seed)` is the race the tests and the attract race use: the player on the Sesta, five rivals on their tier-2 bikes, one cop at 48 m/s with a 40 m sight range, a 1,500 m beat, and a $1,500 fine, tier-2 pacing and tier-1 conditions, and the player last on the grid.

`cast.ts` holds the two officers and builds the sixteen ordinary rivals and six celebrity rivals from the `game` of each row in `packages/content`: name, skill, aggression, home region, the weapon they carry and the tier they carry it from, a bike for each of the three tiers, and their colours. `rivalAt(rival, tier)` turns one into a setup entry and adds the tier's skill. At tier 3 an unarmed rival with aggression of 0.5 or more starts with a club. Their lines, the field draw, and everything else about the career live in `packages/career`. `celebrityModels` names the rider model each celebrity wears. The served race rules turn the celebrities on with `celebrities`, and `recordedRules` leaves them off so the measured balance and the perf races keep their fields. A celebrity rides only on its home road, among that road's home riders, and has no lines, bar figure or poster yet: `rivalLines`, `figures` and `bills` are keyed by `OrdinaryRivalId`, so the compiler asks for each one once a later change writes them. Section 9 of `docs/design/game-design.md` lists the cast.

A profile's `paint` and `jacket` are indices into the eighteen entries of `palette.ts`, which gives the bike, jacket, and helmet colour for each rider and a trim colour where there is one: the gold stripe on Juno Kade and the blue panels on the white police bikes.

### Bike and rider tables

`packages/content` holds one row per bike and per rider, and depends on nothing. A bike row has its `id`, its `spec` (the riding numbers), its `shop` (the tier it arrives at) and its `looks` (the model that draws it and its showroom paint). A rider row has its `id`, its `game` numbers, its short name and pronouns, and either `lines` and `art` for an ordinary rival or a `bio` and the model it `wears` for a celebrity. `visuals.ts` lists the bike and rider models and the GLB file for each.

Each package reads only its part of a row, and the types of that package check it: `packages/sim` reads `spec` and `game`, `packages/career` reads `shop`, `lines` and `bio`, and the browser reads `looks`, `art` and the names. `packages/sim` builds a `BikeSpec` from `spec` alone, so a change to `looks` cannot change how a bike rides.

To add a bike or a rider, add a row, list its new IDs in `packages/career/save-format.json`, and add its art. A new bike also needs a rival who rides it, in that rival's `game.bikes`. Perf scenarios, replays and test fixtures race under `recordedRules`, which names its cast in `recordedCast`, so a new rider moves no baseline. Art names are checked against `apps/web/src/assets/art.json` when the web app compiles. A new mechanic, rig or shader still needs a client release, because rows only configure what the game can already do.

A race never looks a number up in these tables once it starts. `RaceSetup` carries each rider's `BikeSpec` and profile, and `validateSetup` refuses numbers outside the ranges in `packages/sim/src/setup-ranges.ts`, for the player, every rival and the officer. A row there also says whether its value must be a whole number: a paint or jacket index, the police count and the slow convoys are. Each gear's top speed must sit close enough above the gear below that the revs, once the box has shifted up at 96% of the lower gear and drag has slowed the bike through the 8 tick cut, stay above the 58% where it shifts down. `keepsGearAfterUpshift` in `engine.ts` asks the box itself, with the engine's own drag on a seated rider and its own cut. Otherwise a bike such as one geared 10 and 100 m/s shifts between its two gears every tick and never passes 9.6 m/s, and a Wren geared 22.9 and 37.9 m/s, whose upshift lands 0.002 m/s above the downshift point before drag counts, falls back to first three ticks into the cut and hunts at 79 km/h. The types fix the roles: `PlayerSetup` and `RivalSetup` carry a `RacerProfile` and the police an `OfficerProfile`, so only racers are placed and only racers finish. A later race manifest can send the same values through the same check.

## Race snapshots

`packages/sim/src/snapshot.ts` writes a race to bytes, reads it back, and hashes it. One schema holds the field tables as data: `snapshot-members.ts` holds the member codecs and the three walks over a table, `bytesOf`, `putRecord` and `takeRecord`, so the byte count, the writer and the reader are the same list read three ways and a field cannot be written and not restored. `snapshot-format.ts` holds the moving tables, typed `Fields<Read, Write>` over `keyof Write`, so a new field in `RaceState` without a codec entry fails `tsc`, and `packages/sim/src/snapshot-holes.test.ts` pins the five ways a table can lie: a missing field, a field the record lacks, an optional field without an explicit presence codec, a union codec that misses a variant, and a digest-only codec in a table that must restore. `snapshot-bytes.ts` holds the primitives and the write cursor, `snapshot-events.ts` the one tagged codec for every `RaceEvent` kind, `snapshot-setup.ts` the setup digest, and `snapshot-hash.ts` the hash.

The format starts with a 32-byte header: the format word, the snapshot's length, the four words of the setup digest, and the tick. Then come the mutable fields in a fixed order, cars, cross-road cars, dice, events, hazard dice, hazards, pedestrians, pilot history and riders, each with an explicit length where it has one. Every number is a float64 written little-endian through `DataView`, so signed zero and both infinities survive exactly, and a NaN is written as one canonical pattern because ECMAScript leaves `setFloat64`'s NaN bytes to the engine. Text is a counted run of UTF-16 code units and refuses a code point above `0xFFFF`.

The immutable inputs are not copied. `setupDigest(state, track)` hashes the compiled track the simulation reads, the police and pacing rules, and every rider's profile and bike, including the gear ratios, into 128 bits, and the header carries it. The track part is the compiled data itself, not a label: the sampled centreline, curvature, heading, slope and slope rate, the world coordinates, every feature, hazard and prop with its position and radius, and the plan's race settings with its districts, the per-metre widths and open lanes. A car's `home` lane is hashed there too. Its `x` is in neither place, because a restore works it out from the car's `s` and home. A road whose bends moved by 0.001 gives a different digest. A restore refuses a snapshot whose format word, length, rider count, car count or digest does not match, so a snapshot of another road or another field cannot be read into this race.

The API is four calls. `snapshotCapacity(state)` gives the bytes a race needs now, `writeSnapshot(state, digest, out)` writes into the caller's buffer and returns the length, `restoreInto(state, digest, from, track)` reads one back into a race that is already built, and `restoreSnapshot(setup, from)` builds a fresh race and reads into it. `hashRace(state, digest, scratch, into)` writes the snapshot into the caller's scratch buffer and hashes it. Checkpoint N is the state after exactly N completed ticks, which is what `writeSnapshot` records in the header's tick. `restoreInto` allocates nothing in steady state: riders, cars, hazards and pilot history are written in place, and the tick's events are written into the records the destination already holds, kind by kind, so restoring the same combat snapshot twice hands back the same event objects. Each event slot keeps one warmed record per kind, and a hazard tag keeps its record through a snapshot that carries none, so a slot that alternates between a hit and an attack, or a crash whose tag comes and goes, reuses what it already holds. Only a list that has to grow, or a kind a slot has never held, costs one record.

The hash is MurmurHash3 x86 128, Austin Appleby's public-domain mixer, written word for word in TypeScript with `Math.imul` and no `node:crypto`, so it runs in the browser and in a Worker. It replaced a four-lane mixer of this repository's own, which had a systematic collision: when the four lanes' signs agreed, flipping the top bit of one word and adding a fixed difference to the next cancelled in every lane, so two states that differ in two adjacent words hashed the same. `packages/sim/src/snapshot.test.ts` keeps two such pairs, one in a rider's `load` and one in a `finishTick`, and a synthetic compensating pair, and all three fail on the old mixer. A hash needs bitwise operators, so `snapshot-hash.ts` is the one file `rushline/no-bitwise` lists. A 21,808-byte stress snapshot hashes in 0.065 ms at p95 on this Mac, inside the `snapshot.hash.p95` budget, and the hash is a diagnostic, never a vote.

`apps/web/test/pilot-replay.ts` is the only serializer in the repository: it hashes each tick with `hashRace` and chains the per-tick hashes into the `full` and `window` digests in `pilot-replay-baseline.json`. The baseline holds two recorded sets. `solo` rides each of the eleven races to its own terminal outcome, between 4,721 and 21,726 ticks, and records that tick and the outcome beside the hashes. `fixed` keeps the 7,200-tick ride every case was measured over before the terminal rule existed, so the same early ticks stay fingerprinted whatever that rule later decides. Each set's `full` digest chains every tick of its own ride, so a set is checked tick by tick, and one further check holds a case's fixed ride against its ride to the outcome over every tick the two share, which is what makes them two recordings of the same eleven races. The last case, `handover/slag-row/tier3/seed5`, starts the player with a human controller that weaves and punches on a fixed script, hands the first rival to a human at tick 600 and back to the pilot at 1,500, and hands the player to the pilot at 1,800, back to the human at 2,400 and to the pilot for good at 3,000. Every human control in a replay goes through `writeControl` and `readControl` before the tick. `pnpm replay:engines` (`tools/replay`) bundles the same eleven races with esbuild and runs them in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit beside Node, comparing every tick's hash, the tick each race ends on and the outcome it reaches, and naming the first case and tick that differ. Each engine first restores a mid-race checkpoint in a page that has run no tick, and a second Node process does the same, which is the proof that nothing a race leaves in module scratch is needed to carry it on. The `replay` job in `.github/workflows/quality.yml` runs one engine each on every push.
