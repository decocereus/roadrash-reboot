# Renderer standards

Rules for `apps/web/src/game` and its tests. The renderer reads the simulation, so the rules in [simulation.md](simulation.md) that name the renderer apply here too, and so do the core rules in [coding-standards.md](../coding-standards.md).

## Keep each module to its job

| Rule | Check | Scope | Exemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scenery the chase camera can reach gives way to it through the world's clearance in `apps/web/src/game/clearance.ts`, and nothing else hides a prop by its distance from the camera. `plant` in `ground.ts` registers every instanced placement it plants, and a grouped prop such as a gantry post or a finish gate joins with `addObject`. A stall or decoration merged into a market's mesh joins with `addSpans` and hides by zeroing its index range. The clearance measures each part's footprint from its geometry and placement, so a pine counts at the width of its crown and a sign at the width of its board, and hides every part of a placement together while the eye is within 2 m of any of them. Road props once had their own 3 m test at the foot, and chevrons and pines had none, so a rider running wide put the camera inside a sign or a crown (cam-1, October 24, 2026). The band is 2 m because the reproduced chevron and pine sit 1.07 m and 1.66 m from the eye, while a 3 m band hid pine crowns in view on Ridge Road with the rider still on the road. The tests pin both sides. The lint rule rejects writing `visible` or calling `setMatrixAt` where the value written, or a test that decides it, calls `Math.hypot`, `Math.sqrt` or a three distance or length method. It cannot see a distance worked out elsewhere and passed in by name; review checks those, and `apps/web/test/roadside-clearance.test.ts` drives `createWorld` and its `follow` with the real chase camera. | `rushline/no-distance-hide` | `apps/web/src/game/**/*.ts` | None |
| The normal and depth pass in `post.ts` draws each mesh with the side its own material draws, so a board drawn from both sides hides what stands behind it from the outlines as it does from the colour. With the override fixed to the front side, the back of a double-sided chevron or warning board let the outline pass see the rider behind it and ink the rider onto the sign (cam-1). A new pass that overrides materials carries each material's side the same way. `apps/web/test/outline-sides.ts` renders a box behind a double-sided board, from its back and its front, through the real post chain; run it as the roadside clearance recipe says. | judgment | `apps/web/src/game/post.ts` | None |
| Each game releases what it made exactly once, in reverse order, through the owner list in `createGame`, on dispose or when construction throws partway, and a second dispose does nothing. A world owns its scene, textures, shadow map and cel ramp; actors and figures share that ramp and never release it. `disposeTree` releases each shared geometry, material, instanced mesh and point cloud once and leaves textures to their owners. Warm-up puts back everything it staged, even when the draw or the setup throws. | `pnpm test` | `apps/web/src/game`, `apps/web/test/renderer-lifecycle.test.ts` | None |
| Each shader preparation ends in exactly one outcome: `ready`, `timedOut`, `failed` with its stage and cause, `lost` or `cancelled`. It leaves no deadline or poll behind, only the newest preparation warms, nothing warms while the WebGL context is lost, and a late ready after a timeout, a replacement or an unmount does nothing. | `pnpm test` | `apps/web/src/game/preparation.ts`, `apps/web/test/preparation.test.ts` | None |

## Keep the frame loop deterministic

| Rule | Check | Scope | Exemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The HUD publishes one regular snapshot each time 1/15 s of processed frame time builds up: each rendered frame adds its clamped seconds, a total of 1/15 s or more publishes once and resets to zero, and the remainder is dropped. Halted frames add nothing. A race being prepared, the attract loop, a quit, sleep or mute publishes at once, apart from that clock. | `pnpm test` | `apps/web/src/game/hud-cadence.ts`, `apps/web/test/hud-cadence.test.ts` | None |

## Upload and sort only what the frame needs

The renderer runs these paths every frame, so a cost that scales with a buffer's capacity or with each comparison shows up in the frame time. Each rule here was fixed once and then came back.

| Rule | Check | Scope | Exemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Game code marks a buffer attribute for upload with `uploadSpan` in `apps/web/src/game/uploads.ts`, passing the span of items that changed or are drawn. It skips an empty span, counts the range in scalar components rather than items, folds any range the renderer has not yet consumed into one, and sets `needsUpdate`. An attribute that has never been drawn needs no mark, because three's first upload sends the whole array. Bikes uploaded their whole instance buffers every frame until `23f110a`, and the traffic fleet then did the same with its matrices and colours. The lint rule requires every assignment to `needsUpdate` on a buffer attribute, other than `= false`, to come right after an `addUpdateRange` call on the same receiver in the same block. It recognises a buffer attribute by syntax: `.instanceMatrix`, `.instanceColor`, any member of `.attributes`, a `getAttribute(…)` result, a binding initialised with `new` of `BufferAttribute`, `InstancedBufferAttribute` or a typed subclass such as `Float32BufferAttribute`, a binding initialised with any of these, and a binding or parameter annotated with one of those classes. Materials, textures, render targets and the shadow map are none of these. The rule cannot see an attribute reached another way, such as through an object field, a destructured binding, a loop variable or a function's result, or an upload forced through `version`; review checks those. | `rushline/ranged-buffer-upload` | `apps/web/src/game/**/*.ts` | None |
| Warm-up compiles every shader program and uploads every texture a race draws, so nothing a player meets mid-race stalls a frame for it. `stageEverything` in `apps/web/src/game/staging.ts` adds a sample of every city material, attaches every weapon in both hands and primes the particle pools for one render through the real pipeline, and anything new a race can draw, such as a pedestrian, a junction or a sponsor board, joins what it stages. A shared shadow-depth material that compiled a new variant at tick 268 stalled a 125 ms frame (`ba109d2`). `pnpm perf:gpu` fails when the WebGL context creates a program or a texture on any frame after the first of B's two Slag Row races. It sees only what those races draw after the start grid, so a resource that only another road or an effect they do not trigger draws passes and is for review. It does not count geometries, because city chunks stream in and hazard pools grow by design, so a geometry first uploaded mid-race, as each weapon's was before `8f6ebfe`, is for review too. | `pnpm perf:gpu` | `apps/web/src/game` | None |
| A comparator passed to `setOpaqueSort` or `setTransparentSort` compares numbers the render item already carries, such as `material.id`, and computes nothing per comparison. three calls it for every pair it compares, every frame. Comparing material uuid strings took 1.5 to 10 percent of the main thread's self time (`009383f`), a `WeakMap` numbering of materials brought a hash lookup back on both sides of every comparison (`f25cd7b`), and `81a7cf7` fixed both by reading three's numeric material id. The lint rule reads the comparator, which must be an inline function, `null`, or a `function` or `const` function declared in the same file, and every same-file function it calls by name, transitively. In them it rejects reading `uuid`, by member or destructuring, and calling `localeCompare`, `get`, `has`, `find`, `findIndex`, `indexOf`, `includes`, `sort`, `toSorted`, `map`, `filter` or `reduce`, including through static computed keys. It also rejects a call by name to anything else the file binds, such as an import, because it cannot read it. It cannot see what a method called on an object does, or other array walks such as `some` or `findLast`; review checks those. | `rushline/numeric-draw-sort` | `apps/web/src/game/**/*.ts` | None |

## Test what a player would notice

| Rule | Check | Scope | Exemption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| After a change to what the game creates or releases, run `apps/web/test/renderer-lifecycle.ts` in an `rv up --dev` slot at `/kit?prof` with `rv js 'import("/test/renderer-lifecycle.ts").then((m) => m.run())'`. Its geometry, texture and program counts must return to their warmed values after each track change (A, B, A) and each quality change (lowest rung, top rung, lowest rung), with no upward trend, and three real `WEBGL_lose_context` cycles must end `lost` and then prepare `ready` again. A reload whose warm-up draw throws must still release the retired actors, and the retired world as well when the road changes. A race started while its own preparation is still dressing must count down on its own road. | judgment | `apps/web/test/renderer-lifecycle.ts` | None |
