# Race room

A race room runs one race for up to eight humans on the `RaceServer` Durable Object in `apps/rooms`. It holds WebSocket seats, steps at 60 Hz and sends every seat each tick. A browser joins with `/?room=<room id>`. `pnpm rooms:race` races scripted seats through the same `openRoomSeat` client the browser uses, each one a real `createRoomClient` over a real WebSocket that predicts, rolls back and compares hashes with the room.

## Sub-features

- `tick-rate` holds 60 Hz: a seat's arrival gaps sit near 16.7 ms and it confirms at least 97% of the expected ticks.
- `agreement` keeps every seat on the room's race: `digestMatched` is true and `desyncs` is 0 with `hashesCompared` above 0.
- `no-missed-inputs` gets a seat's controls in on time. `missed` in the status counts the ticks the room stepped with no control from that seat, and it is 0 or close to it on a local link. `pnpm rooms:race` fails when a seat misses over 2% of the ticks. `late` counts every copy of a control that arrived after its tick, and a batch resends up to 8 controls, so it overstates what a rider loses.
- `seat-refusals` sends a ninth seat `refused` with `full`, seats a member who arrives after the start in the lobby for the next race, and answers a plain request to a seat with 426 and a malformed room ID with 404.
- `own-room` gives each room ID its own room: another room ID reads as a waiting room with no seats while this race runs.
- `dropped-seat` holds a closed seat for the room's seat hold, 30 s at launch, while the pilot rides it and the others ride on, then leaves it with the pilot.
- `vanished-seat` drops a seat the room has heard nothing from for 10 s, as a friend losing Wi-Fi looks, writes its `left` line, and the others ride on.
- `lobby-stays-awake` starts a race after the lobby sat longer than the 10 s a Durable Object needs to hibernate. Its fill and deadline timers keep it in memory.
- `fair-start` holds the countdown until every seat has loaded the race, for at most 20 s, so a slow browser does not miss the start. A seat reads `Loading the race` until then.
- `failed-load` takes a rider whose race failed to dress or build out of the race and back to the same room's lobby, with no ready packet, and the other riders' countdown does not wait for them.
- `boot-failure` shows a rider whose game failed to boot `Could not start the game:` and the message, with Reload and Leave, in place of the lobby. That rider holds no seat and sends no ready packet, so the other riders' countdown does not wait for them, and a seat that was open closes with 4410, so the lobby drops them at once instead of listing them `AWAY`.
- `leave-at-door` lets a rider close the page during the door moment. The seat leaves with no ready packet, and the other riders' countdown does not wait for them. The door moment has no Leave button, so closing or navigating the page is the only way out.
- `late-load` starts a slow rider's race after the room's 20 s deadline: the room races on, and that rider reports loaded and joins the race as soon as its own race is built.
- `lobby-names` lists the humans in the lobby by name, with any fill as `and <n> more`, so a rider can see a friend arrive.
- `race-tape` saves every race's packets to R2, and `pnpm rooms:replay` runs the race again and matches every state hash.
- `race-trace` saves each browser's timing timeline and the room's missed controls next to the tape, and `pnpm rooms:trace` lines them up by second.
- `queue` forms a public lobby of up to eight riders, at once when the eighth arrives or when the oldest ticket has waited 30 s. It runs on one `QueueServer` Durable Object, which refuses an older build. `pnpm rooms:queue --url <rooms url>` fails when a rider who left stays queued, when eight riders do not land in one public room, when a lone rider leaves early or at tier or bike other than the public rule, or when an old build gets in.
- A public lobby (a `pub-` room ID) has no host, the server picks its road for each race, and it leaves 45 s after the first rider arrives, or 10 s after if everyone is ready.
- `lobby` keeps its members, host, rules and ready state across races, and starts a race when every member is ready or a minute after the first rider readied, so a friend still on results gets that minute to come back. No player has a start button.
- `lost-room` shows a window that loses the room mid-race `Signal lost` with the hold counting down, and reloads into the same room as the same member.
- `rejoin` puts a rider who comes back inside the hold on the same bike, caught up to the room's tick through catch-up packets, with `Your bike is up` and their place for 3 s. A rider back after the hold sees `We kept your bike` in the lobby. `Back to the bar` gives the bike up at once. `pnpm exec vitest run --project node apps/rooms/test/host-rejoin.test.ts` pins the rules, and the two-window check below shows the screens.
- `friends-link` opens a new room from `Race friends` on the bar. `Copy the link` copies a URL that seats a second window in the same room.
- `guest-names` asks for a name once per browser, keeps it in `rushline.guestName.v1`, and shows each human's name on the other humans' result sheets in place of a field name. The server cleans every name, so a tampered URL cannot send markup or more than 12 characters.
- `room-logs` writes a few lines per race instead of one per message, because a log event costs far more than the message it records.
- `race-id` gives each race a random ID, separate from the room's invite link, shared by every seat. `pnpm rooms:race` fails when the seats disagree.
- `race-report` turns one race's log lines into a readable report with `pnpm rooms:read`.
- `load-test` runs real seats against many rooms, with load recorded and a message cap on the real network, and reports how many rooms one thread holds.
- `back-to-lobby` returns a rider from room results to the same lobby and rules with `Back to the lobby`. `Continue` goes back to the bar with no room in the URL.
- `net-line` prints round trip, rollbacks, resimulated ticks per second and input to frame on room results. `globalThis.__rushlineRoom.report()` returns the same numbers mid-race.
- `seat-phases` moves a seat only through `seatAfter`. `ended`, `left`, `lost` and `refused` are final, so a late message or a later `leave` changes nothing.
- `host-phases` moves the room only through `hostAfter`, and the status route's `hostPhase` reads `loading`, `countdown`, `racing` and `waiting` while its `phase` keeps the two values a tab on the previous deploy can decode.
- `socket-roles` moves a socket only through `roleAfter`. Each socket is `lobby`, `seated` in the race, or `reporting` for up to 5 s after it ends, and the status route's `roles` lists them in the order of `names`.
- `race-end` ends the race and closes every seat with 1000 once every rider is done.
- `time-limit` hands every seat to the pilot at six minutes, so a rider who stops and leaves the tab open still gets a result and the room ends.
- `browser-race` puts two windowed browsers in one race, each riding its own bike, with the other human on its road.

## Entry points

- `RUSHLINE_WEB_PORT=<port> RUSHLINE_ROOMS_PORT=<port> pnpm exec alchemy dev --stage verify` runs the Worker in local workerd with no Cloudflare login. Use free ports from 1420 up, never 1350 or 1351.
- `pnpm rooms:race --url <rooms url>` races scripted seats and fails on any problem it lists.
- `pnpm rooms:load --url <rooms url> --rooms 2,4,8` finds how many rooms one thread holds. `pnpm rooms:lag --listen <port> --target <port>` puts a slow, lossy link in front of a room.
- `pnpm rooms:fights --rooms-port <port> --out <dir>` races two real windows, each through its own `rooms:lag` link, and checks every strike from the other rider's window.
- `rv throttle --network` does not slow the race. Chrome's network emulation leaves the game's WebSocket alone, and `__rushlineRoom.rttMs()` stays at 1 to 2 ms under `slow4g` and `3g`. Use `rooms:lag` for any multiplayer network test, and read `rttMs()` to confirm the link is real.

## Recipes

Start the Worker and leave it running. `pnpm rooms:race` prints one JSON report and exits non-zero with its `problems`.

- **Tick rate, agreement, inputs, refusals, own room, dropped seat.** Run `pnpm rooms:race --url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --seats 8 --seconds 25 --drop-at-seconds 20`. `problems` is empty. Seven reports show about 1,500 `ticks`, the eighth stops near 1,200 with `closeCode` 1000, `status.seats` lists seven riders each with `late` 0, and `worstBehindTicks` is 1 or 2.
- **Vanished seat.** Put a TCP proxy in front of the rooms port that keeps the upstream socket open and silent when its client dies, seat one scripted rider through it and one directly, start, then `kill -9` the proxied rider. Within about 11 s `curl <rooms url>/rooms/<id>` reads `waiting` 1, the log has a `left` line for that rider, and the other rider's `missed` stays 0.
- **Lobby stays awake.** Run `pnpm rooms:race --url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --seats 2 --seconds 10`. It waits out the 60 s lobby. `problems` is empty, which shows the sockets and timers survived past the hibernation window.
- **Live game and rooms agree.** Alchemy rebuilds the web bundle only when its input files change, and an env value is not an input, so `deploy.yml` writes the commit to `apps/web/public/rushline-build.txt` first and fails if the live site does not serve that commit back. After a deploy, open the live game in a room and sit down. A mismatch refuses every seat.
- **A far seat.** Run `node tools/rooms/lag.ts --listen 1443 --target <rooms port> --rtt 1000`, then `pnpm rooms:race --url http://127.0.0.1:1443 --seats 2 --seconds 25`. `problems` stays empty and neither rider rides a tick with no control. Add `--rtt 300 --burst-every 10 --burst-ms 1000` for a bursty link: each burst costs a few misses, because the clock raises its lead when the burst's ping or lost controls arrive and lets the raise fade over 2 s. Add `--upload-ms 150 --upload-after 30` to slow only the uplink 30 s in.
- **Lag tag.** Run `node tools/rooms/lag.ts --listen 1443 --target <rooms port> --rtt 300 --burst-every 12 --burst-ms 3000`, start with `VITE_ROOMS_URL=http://127.0.0.1:1443 rv up --dev --path "/?room=<id>"`, and ride with real keys while steering, because `&autopilot` sends no controls. `[data-slot=dial] [role=status]` reads `Lag` for about 1.5 s after each stall and is gone between them. With `--burst-ms 1000` it stays off.
- **Fights on every link.** Start the Worker, then run `pnpm rooms:fights --rooms-port <port> --out <dir> --links direct,home,mobile,far,bursts,upload --slots 3,4`. Each link is a pair of `rooms:lag` settings, one per window: `home` is 110 ms with 15 ms jitter on both, `mobile` 180 ms with 60 ms jitter and 1% loss on both, `far` puts the second window at 300 ms against 110 ms, `bursts` adds a 1 s stall every 10 s and 1% loss to that 300 ms seat, and `upload` slows only the second window's uplink by 150 ms, 30 s after the link opens. For each link the tool seats both windows in a new room, starts the race, steers the riders alongside each other, and has each rider kick and punch the other. Each window steers from the race it draws, as a player does, not from the room's confirmed race, which runs a lead and a one-way trip behind. A link's `rooms:lag` proxy keeps one queue per direction for all its connections, so a connection opened after another never reaches the room first; a dev build opens the seat socket twice on Join, and without that order a jittery link let the aborted first socket take the seat and leave the window reading "You opened this lobby in another tab." If a rider's bike is wrecked, the remaining bouts stop at once and say so, because that race can't bring them alongside again. It records the victim's window for each strike and then kicks the second rider down. `<dir>/table.md` has one row per strike, and every time in it comes from the two windows sharing the Mac's clock. A strike passes when the room ran it, the victim's screen drew it fully out, and the victim's shove and flinch showed no earlier than that. The shove is the first frame where the victim's guessed race shows the attacker's strike landing, with strike stun or on the tick the room's hit landed, so a bump between the riders never counts as one. A strike that knocks the victim off the bike passes without a flinch. The stage columns split the delay: press to the input packet, that packet to the victim's confirm of the strike, the room's hold (the attacker's own confirm minus the packet and the round trip at the press), the victim's confirm to the strike being drawn, and press to the strike fully out. "Presses the room dropped" counts confirmed ticks that carried the attacker's control without the button it sent. "Attacker drawn off the room's place at the hit" compares the attacker's drawn position, on the frame the victim's guessed race shows the hit, with where the room had the attacker on that tick. The pilot weaves rider 1 0.3 m either side of its lane every 2.4 s, so both windows draw a rider whose steering they must guess. `<network>.json` has `tracks`, one per window for the other rider: on every frame where both the guessed race and the room have that rider riding, `guessed` compares the guessed position with where the room had the rider on that tick, and `drawn` compares the position the cast drew that frame with the room's positions for the same two ticks blended the same way (`errorP95Metres`), and `worstStepMetres` is the biggest jump between two such frames in a row. `guessed` is what a client without settling would draw, so one race compares both. `<network>.json` also lists every fall with its cause, for when the riders never come alongside. The tool does not cover weapon swings, steals or police.
- **Fights on production.** Run `pnpm rooms:fights --server <game url> --out <dir> --slots 3,4`. Both windows open the live game through `rv up --server --user-asked`, with no lag proxies, so the rows read `live` and carry each window's real round trip. Every merge deploys, so check that nobody is racing on production first. Only multiplayer checks use this; vimaan takes no seats.
- **Old build refused.** `pnpm rooms:race` opens one seat that claims another build. `problems` stays empty only if that seat reads `build`, and the room's log has a `refused` line with reason `build`. In a browser the room screen reads `The game has updated. Reload to join.` with a Reload item.
- **Lobby.** `pnpm exec vitest run --project node apps/rooms/test/lobby.test.ts` covers the rules. In two windows on one room: the first arrives through `Race friends` and is `HOST`, and `You set the rules` opens the rule choices. The second opens the link, reads the rules and presses `Join`, and `The rules` shows them in the bar. Pick a road and traffic in the first and read them in the second. Press `Ready` in one, `Rolling out in <n> s, ready or not.` appears, and a rule change clears it. Press `Ready` in both and the race starts at once on the chosen road.
- **Dead room.** Start a two-window race, then restart the Worker with `touch apps/rooms/src/worker.ts` under `alchemy dev`. Both windows load `/?room=<id>` and read the lobby, with the default rules. A window whose socket hangs with no close does the same after 5 s of silence.
- **Race end.** Run `pnpm rooms:race --url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --seats 2 --seconds 400`. It takes a few minutes. Both reports show `ended` true and `closeCode` 1000, and `status.phase` is `waiting` with the lobby still holding its rule.
- **Fair start.** Start the Worker, put a TCP delay proxy in front of the rooms port for one window through `VITE_ROOMS_URL`, seat both windows, and wait for the first window to reach the name screen before opening the second, because two Vite servers starting together can leave a page on the boot screen. In the slow window run `setTimeout(() => { const t = Date.now(); while (Date.now() - t < 6000); }, 150)`, then `rv click --run <other> "Start the race"`. The other window reads `Loading the race` for those 6 s and neither window reports `predicted.tick` above 0 until about 9 s after the click. `client.confirmed.tick` is 0 at each window's first predicted tick. A window on a 350 ms link goes green about 170 ms before a window on a local link, because its controls for tick 1 must leave earlier to reach the room in time.
- **Failed load.** Run **Two browsers** with `&as=kylie` on the first window, which makes its race download that celebrity, and `&autopilot` on the second. Read the model's file name from `apps/web/src/assets/art.json`, then keep a DevTools session open on the first window's port that sends `Network.enable` and `Network.setBlockedURLs` with `urlPatterns` `[{ "urlPattern": "*://*:*/art/<file name>", "block": true }]`. The block lasts only while that session stays attached, and `fetch("/art/<file name>")` in the page should reject. Press `Ready` in both. In that session's `Network.webSocketFrameSent` events, the first window sends no binary frame whose first byte is 6, the ready packet, and its page navigates to `/?room=<id>&as=kylie&via=moved` within a second. The bar there reads `A race is running. You ride the next one.` The Worker's Rooms log under `.alchemy/log/verify/Rooms` has a `left` line for that rider at `tick` 0, then a `countdown` line with `waitedMs` about 5000, the second window's door moment, and not the 20 s deadline. Close the session to lift the block, wait for the race to end, press `Ready` in both, and the first window rides the next race.
- **Boot failure.** Start two `rv up` windows with `VITE_ROOMS_URL` and set `localStorage` `rushline.guestName.v1` in each, so `&via=made` goes straight to the room. Read `models/car.glb`'s file name from `apps/web/src/assets/art.json`. Keep a DevTools session open on the first window's port that sends `Network.enable`, `Network.setBlockedURLs` with `urlPatterns` `[{ "urlPattern": "*://*:*/art/<file name>", "block": true }]`, and then `Page.navigate` to `/?room=<id>&via=made`, so the block covers the boot downloads. The first window reads `Could not start the game: Failed to fetch` with Reload and Leave, its session sees no `Network.webSocketCreated`, and `curl <rooms url>/rooms/<id>` lists only the second window once it opens the room. Press `Ready` there: the Rooms log's `countdown` has `waitedMs` about 5000. For a boot that fails after the rider sat down, hold the model with `Fetch.enable` and `patterns` `[{ "urlPattern": "*/art/<file name>" }]` instead, press `Ready` in both, then answer every `Fetch.requestPaused` with `Fetch.failRequest`, including the loader's one retry, which arrives a few milliseconds after the first answer. Within a second the first window reads the same line and sends no binary frame whose first byte is 6, and the Rooms log has a `left` line for that rider at `tick` 0 with `closeCode` 4410, then a `countdown` a few milliseconds later with an empty `stillLoading`; its `waitedMs` is the time from the start to the failure, or about 5000 if the failure came first. Open the second window on `/?room=<id>&via=moved`: its lobby lists only itself, with no `AWAY` rider. On a build without the fix the first window sits in the bar, then reads `Rolling out.`, and the `countdown` has `waitedMs` about 20000 with that rider in `stillLoading`.
- **Leave frees the spot.** `pnpm exec vitest run --project node apps/rooms/test/host-leave.test.ts` pins the rule. Then put a TCP proxy in front of the rooms port that, on a signal, stops forwarding while it keeps every socket open and refuses new ones, and on a second signal accepts new ones again. Run **Two browsers** with the first window's `VITE_ROOMS_URL` on the proxy port and name both. Press `Leave` in the first: the second window's member list drops that name within about 300 ms and the next member reads `HOST`. Open the first window on `/?room=<id>&via=moved` again, silence the proxy, and the second window reads that name as `AWAY` after about 11 s and keeps it there. Let the proxy accept again: within a second the name reads `NOT READY` again, in the same colour and place in the list.
- **A new tab takes back the spot.** `pnpm exec vitest run --project node apps/web/test/member-key.test.ts` pins the key. Then run **Two browsers** in one room, named through `localStorage` `rushline.guestName.v1` and `&via=made`. Close the first window's tab through its DevTools port (`curl <devtools>/json/close/<target id>`) and open the invite link in a new tab of the same browser (`curl -X PUT "<devtools>/json/new?<web url>/?room=<id>"`). The second window lists that name `AWAY`. Press `Join` in the new tab: the second window lists the name `NOT READY` again in the same colour and place, `HOST` back on it if it was the host, and no second row for it. On a build that keeps the key in sessionStorage the second window keeps the `AWAY` row and adds the name again in a new colour. Open the link in another tab while that one is live and press `Join`: the older tab reads `You opened this lobby in another tab.` and stays there, the room still lists the name once, and `Leave` in the older tab returns it to the bar without freeing the seat.
- **Leave at the door.** Run **Two browsers** and keep a DevTools session open on the first window's port with `Network.enable`. Press `Ready` in both, wait about 1.5 s, and check no `button` on the page reads `Leave`. Run `rv open --run <first> /`. In that session's `Network.webSocketFrameSent` events the first window sent no binary frame whose first byte is 6. The Rooms log has a `left` line for that rider at `tick` 0, then a `countdown` line with `waitedMs` about 5000, and the second window reaches the race.
- **Late load.** Run **Two browsers** with `&as=kylie` on the first window, and keep a DevTools session open on its port that sends `Fetch.enable` with `patterns` `[{ "urlPattern": "*/art/<file name>" }]` and holds each `Fetch.requestPaused`. Press `Ready` in both. The second window reaches the race at the room's deadline, and the Rooms log's `countdown` line has `waitedMs` about 20000 and the first rider in `stillLoading`. The first window has heard the `countdown` message and has no speed meter and no ready packet. Send `Fetch.continueRequest`. The first window sends its ready packet and reaches the race within a few seconds, with `client.stats.desyncs` 0 in both.
- **Room logs.** On the deployed Worker, `GET /accounts/<id>/workers/scripts/<rooms script>/settings` shows `invocation_logs` false. After a race, the rooms Worker's events in Workers Logs are a few JSON lines per race: `started` with the race ID, build, road, seed and digest, `countdown` with `waitedMs` and the rider numbers in `stillLoading`, `left`, `refused`, one `stats` line per rider from the browser, kept by the room as they arrive every 15 s and written when the rider's socket closes or the race ends, with no line from a dropped connection, and `ended`, which sums up every rider who started. There are no per-message events and no names.
- **Race report.** Under `alchemy dev`, save the output to a file and run `pnpm rooms:race` against it, then `pnpm rooms:read --file <log> --last 1`. It prints the race ID, the build, the lobby and load times, how many browsers reported, the gaps between wakes and each rider's numbers. For a deployed race, set `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` and run `pnpm rooms:read --last 3`, or add `--race <race id>`. To test the build label, start `alchemy dev` with `RUSHLINE_BUILD=<any text>`, and the report names it.
- **Race tape.** Under `alchemy dev`, run a race with `pnpm rooms:race`. The local bucket is under `.alchemy/local/r2`, where the `_mf_objects` table of its SQLite file maps each `tapes/<race ID>/` key to a file in `blobs/`. Copy `header.json`, the `.bin` parts and `end.json` into one folder under those names. For a deployed race, get the bucket's name from `cf r2 buckets list`, list the keys under `tapes/<race ID>/` with `cf r2 objects list`, and save each one with `cf r2 objects get`. Then run `pnpm rooms:replay --dir <folder>`. It should print `All <n> state hashes match`, and each rider's mode, finish tick and road should equal the `ended` note from `pnpm rooms:read`. Check out the build the header names first, because other rules refuse the tape. If a part failed to save, the replay names the missing part and fails, and the raw log has a `tapeLost` line with its key.
- **Race trace.** Run a real browser race, because scripted seats draw no frames and send no trace. Download the race's folder as in **Race tape** and run `pnpm rooms:trace --dir <folder>`. It should name each rider's build, graphics card and quality rung, count stalls and tick gaps, and list the bad seconds. `pnpm rooms:read` shows the same browser line and the frame count, p50, p95 and p99 for that rider. With Race data off, that rider has no `rider-<n>.trace.json` and no browser line.
- **Capacity.** Find workerd's own port, because the port in `RUSHLINE_ROOMS_PORT` is a dev proxy that skews the numbers: `pgrep workerd`, then `lsof -a -p <pid> -iTCP -sTCP:LISTEN`, and curl each port's `/rooms/probe-1` for `catchUpWakes`. Run `pnpm rooms:load --url http://127.0.0.1:<port> --rooms 12,16,20 --seats 8 --full-seats 0`. Each step prints one JSON line with `counted`, `held`, `problems` and the load. A step with `counted` false says nothing, so run it again on a calm machine. The last line gives `mostHeldRooms`.
- **Real network.** Show the owner `pnpm rooms:load --url <deployed url> --rooms 8 --seats 4 --seconds 60 --most-messages <cap> --plan` first. Any run on Cloudflare needs the owner's approval, because the account also runs Faceless. Without `--plan` it runs and stops at the cap.
- **Bad network.** Run `pnpm rooms:lag --listen 1439 --target <workerd port>`, then `pnpm rooms:race --url http://127.0.0.1:1439 --seats 4 --seconds 30`. `desyncs` stays 0 and only the `missed` problems may appear. For a look, start the windows with `VITE_ROOMS_URL=http://127.0.0.1:1439` and read `globalThis.__rushlineRoom.report()`.
- **Time limit.** Open one window on `/?room=<id>`, name it, press `Join` and `Ready`, and touch nothing. `curl <rooms url>/rooms/<id>` shows `tick` climbing while the bike sits still. At tick 21,600 the bike rides off by itself, `client.stats.rollbacks` does not jump, and the result sheet appears when the pilot finishes, within about 10 minutes of the start. The status then reads `phase` `waiting`.
- **Two browsers.** Export `VITE_ROOMS_URL=http://127.0.0.1:<rooms port>`, then run `rv up --dev --path "/?room=<id>"` twice with the same `<id>`. `curl <rooms url>/rooms/<id>` reads `waiting` 2. Press `Join` and then `Ready` in each, and the room starts the race at once. Hold `ArrowUp` in each. `globalThis.__rushlineRoom` in each window has a different `me` and `client.stats.desyncs` 0, and `client.confirmed.riders` shows one `bike.id` for every rider with `profile.role` `racer`, and `curl <rooms url>/rooms/<id>` shows `late` 0 for both seats. Each screen centres on the bike at that window's own `client.predicted.riders[me].x`, and the second window, with `me` 1, must not show the bike that crossed.
- **A friend's path.** On a window at `/`, `rv click --run <first> "Race friends"`. - **Public queue.** `pnpm exec vitest run --project node apps/rooms/test/queue.test.ts` covers the rules, and `pnpm rooms:queue --url <rooms url>` runs them against a live Worker. In two windows, press `Find a race` in the clubhouse. Reading the numbers: `rollbacks` and `resimulatedTicks` count every remote steer change the client guessed wrong, so scripted weaving seats roll back several times a second by design. `retriedPresses` counts the button presses the room rode none of the ticks of, which the client then sent again. Each one may have shown its presser the same strike twice, so it stays near zero on a steady link.

## What this does not prove

- A deployed room. Cloudflare's scheduling and `locationHint` placement are untested here. Point `--url` at the deployed Worker to test them.
- Real network lag, jitter or loss. A local link hides lag, and a clock that led by half the round trip passed every local run. A throwaway TCP proxy adding 100 and 200 ms round trips gave `late` 0 and 0 desyncs, and that clock lost every control at 100 ms. No lag tool is checked in yet.
- How a rollback looks over a real network. On a local link the other rider's largest frame-to-frame sideways move was 7.1 cm over 88 rollbacks.
- Public Quick Race matchmaking. Rooms are joined by link only.
- Input to screen. `Input to frame` runs from the key event to the start of the frame after the one that drew the change. It does not include the compositor or the display, and a gamepad has no key event, so a gamepad rider reads 0 samples.
